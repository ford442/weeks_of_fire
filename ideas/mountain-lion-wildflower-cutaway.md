# Mountain Lion Wildflower Field

POV musical cutaway. A mountain lion on a leash **insists** on showing us a field instead of whatever we were doing. Flowers escalate from real meadow scale into botanical surrealism (impossible variety, oversized blooms, some painted), **give way to open ocean**, and come back bigger and stranger on the far shore. Arrival: a sunlit clearing already full of other mountain lions looking at the flowers and the light.

**Kind:** musical cutaway (short packet, not a long HOA scene)

## The ocean act (2026-09-05)

The pull is now **flowers → ocean → flowers again → clearing**. The flowers are the corridor;
the sea is the doorway. Whatever the cat wanted to show us is on the other side of it, and the
cat knew that the whole time.

**The ocean is the crossfade.** `Come Further` runs out as the field thins into water, the 8–12s
fade happens over open sea, and `Here They Are` starts as the blooms come back on the far side.
No third bed to generate — the water sits exactly where the two takes were already going to
overlap. Runtime moves to **~2:20**.

How the water behaves (locked, because this is where it can go wrong):

- **No swimming, no drowning, no peril.** The cat never drags anyone under. Tone stays gratifying.
- **The flowers become the water** instead of stopping at it — petals thin into foam, the last
  lupines stand in shallow surf, the tideline reads as one substance.
- **We cross on something that should not hold**: a causeway of packed floating blooms, a sandbar
  wearing flowers, a kelp path. It holds. Nobody comments.
- **The lion is not impressed by the ocean.** Same walk, same deadpan, wet paws at most.
- **The far shore is not the same field.** Flowers come back larger, stranger, more painted.
  Crossing changed the vocabulary.

Quiet humor for the water: one shake-off at the worst moment for the camera · a single blossom
rides alongside the whole crossing and beaches itself first · the leash floats and nobody adjusts
for it · the cat scratches at wet sand as if to bury something, gets bored, walks on.

## Locked choices

- **POV leash.** Camera is the walker being pulled. Leash reads in frame. Do not cut to an unexplained third-person hero unless expanding later.
- **Deadpan lion.** The cat never acknowledges the absurdity. Focused “this is my walk.” Humor is dignity vs scale.
- **Two-bed fade, same BPM (110), and the fade happens over the ocean.** Do not run 2 minutes of drag + 1 minute of field. Target **~2:20** total (was ~1:50–2:15 before the ocean act).
- **Not metal.** Snake-battle guitar stays on that cutaway. This bed is careful digital FM chirps + minimal soft kick, then brilliant light on arrival.
- **Botanical surrealism, not horror.** No gore, no attack, no “predator jumps the camera” gag. Other lions lounge. They are already here.
- **Do not dump this onto #42** (Ep4 production hub). This is a short cutaway packet.

## Paint rivulets (2026-09-05)

Thin wet enamel colors run in the dirt **between** the wildflowers — ultramarine, sap green, cadmium, alizarin — pooling in ruts and paw prints, never climbing the petals. Same ground language as Daisy Bell's field bookends. Full lock: `ideas/wildflower-paint-rivulets.md`.

Do not collapse this into the painted-petal toolkit. Watercolor bloom edges are one layer. Rivulets in the soil are another. If the model paints the flowers instead of the dirt, the plate failed.

At the tideline the rivulets thin into tint in the foam. No enamel slick on open water. Far shore comes back with thicker, stranger rivulets to match the bigger blooms. One yellow paw print that nobody cares about is enough humor.

This is not the whipped-cream ocean and not Daisy's indoor river. The lion still crosses a real sea.

## Visual arc

1. **Insistence** — lion looks back (or keeps walking) with “you’re coming.” Realistic park / trees. Purple lupines starting to read large. Faint paint in the dirt, easy to miss.
2. **Deeper pull** — multi-color oversized lupines, motion, golden light, leash taut.
3. **Botanical surrealism** — unreal number of species; some blooms photoreal, some painted / watercolor edges; flowers as architecture / tunnel. Rivulets denser underfoot.
4. **The field becomes the sea** — density thins, gold goes silver, the last giants stand in surf, petals and foam become one substance. Paint becomes tint.
5. **The crossing** — open water on a path that should not hold. Widest, quietest, strangest part of the piece. Crossfade lives here.
6. **Far shore** — flowers return at the waterline, bigger and more painted than the ones we left. Gold comes back. Rivulets return thicker.
7. **Arrival** — sun-drenched clearing; other mountain lions already there; original lion still on the leash in the foreground.
8. **Gratifying hold** — light almost stuns; cats + big field; no more pulling; leash goes slack.

## Alternate destinations (totally other ideas)

If the multi-lion clearing ever stops being the payoff. One per cut — the joke dies if the
destination is a list.

- **The other lions, but they are waiting on us.** Every cat already facing the direction we
  arrive from. They have been waiting a while. Nothing else changes.
- **One specific flower.** The whole drag was to show us a single ordinary daisy, correctly sized,
  growing alone. The cat sits down next to it.
- **The edge of the painted part.** A seam where the photoreal field ends and the watercolor one
  begins, and it is a physical edge you can stand at. The cat crosses it. We are still holding
  the leash.
- **The ocean itself was the destination** and the far-shore flowers are a consolation. Ends on
  the cat at the waterline, looking out, done pulling. Quieter, sadder, very good.
- **Where the light comes from.** Follow it to its source and it is just more field, brighter. No
  object, no reveal. The cat is satisfied. We do not get an explanation.

## Botanical surrealism toolkit (for agents expanding stills)

1. Hybrid media — photoreal petals next to painted / watercolor blooms in the same frame.
2. Impossible taxonomy — too many species at once.
3. Scale as architecture — stems as columns, petals as rooms, path as tunnel.
4. Soft biomorphism — optional, quiet; no cartoon faces unless asked.
5. Light as generative force — golden light that seems to grow the painted sections.
6. Chance texture — soft pressed / scraped petal edges on the painted set.
7. Ground rivulets — wet pigment between stems; see shared paint file. Separate from item 1.

Reusable Imagine prompt:

```
Cinematic botanical surrealism, immersive dense field overflowing with an almost impossible number of different oversized flowers of every type. Some fully photoreal, others with delicate painted watercolor and soft brush-stroked edges mixed in the same scene. Towering lupines, bluebells, poppies, roses, foxgloves, lilies, dahlias and fantastical blooms forming a surrounding living architecture / tunnel. Golden sunlight that feels generative, rich textures, dreamlike yet grounded, Weeks on Fire visual style. Thin wet rivulets of enamel-like pigment in ultramarine, sap green, cadmium, and alizarin running along the dirt between the stems, pooling in ruts, never climbing the petals.
```
