# Daisy Bell — the girl at the end

Late focus. A little girl is likely the real Daisy Bell. She does not steal the ride. She receives the name the song has been using.

## Who she is

About seven to ten. Ordinary. Not a mini-Rubella, not a mini-Lillith, not lace, not red lips. Field clothes or a plain 1890s pinafore if she is first seen in London — same face either way. Dark or mid-brown hair, slightly messy. She looks at things the way a child looks at a bicycle, not the way the landau looks at the singers.

She is the namesake. She is not the proposal. “You’ll look sweet upon the seat” does not land on her as romance. If she sits on anything it is an ordinary small bicycle of her own, or the grass.

---

## What she is not

- A third rider on the living tandem.
- A ghost, a double of the ladies, or a time-traveler speech.
- Glowing, crowned in daisies, or announced with a title card.
- The HAL / Bell Labs footnote. C17 can *become* her small bicycle bell. That replaces the computer wink; it does not stack with it.
- Sexualized, stylized-adult, or “cute gothic.” Reject any still that ages her up or puts her in their lace.

## How this talks to the spine

Rubella and Lillith borrowed a bicycle built for two and a song with someone else’s name on it. The field gives the name back. Partnership stays theirs. The title was never theirs.

Kids in the audience get a person. Adults get the receipt. The stuck-guy watch gets a cutaway that was pretty the whole time and then belongs to a child, which is a different hostage.

Do not explain it in dialogue. No one says “Daisy.”
