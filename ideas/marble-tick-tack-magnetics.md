The orbit pass got magnets moving *around* things. This pass is what the takes still do not
have: magnets moving **around each other**, magnets **crossing empty space to hit each other**,
and a lot more of the lot **vibrating on the grid** while they do it.

Three additions, one rule.

1. **Cylinders join the body list.** Discs and rods, which spin and precess in ways spheres cannot.
2. **The jump gap.** A magnet holds across a visible gap, then crosses it in one frame and slams.
3. **The dancer bank.** Far more objects hopping on the eighths, so the rooms vibrate instead of the props.

**Rule: dancers hop, magnets orbit.** Furniture never travels. If a fence slat starts orbiting
something, kill the take.

## Tempo (unchanged, restated because everything below snaps to it)

| Feel | BPM | Period | Use |
|------|-----|--------|-----|
| Tick / cut grid | **136** | 0.441s quarter / 0.220s eighth | Hop, shiver, spin rate, rebound ring |
| Kick / slump | **68** | 0.882s | Coils on, tower stands, **every jump lands here** |
| Ignore | ~92 | 3:2 grouping | Do not snap to it |

One revolution per beat = **136 RPM**. Watchable, fast, not planets. A jump every kick is too
many — **one jump per clip, two at the absolute most**, or it stops being an event.

## New jobs

Say the job. Do not lecture the model about physics.

| Job | What it looks like | Fails as |
|-----|--------------------|----------|
| **Mutual orbit** | Two equal bodies rotate around the **empty point between them** — no center object, no support | A marble bowl; one big thing with a moon |
| **End-over-end tumble** | A rod pair rotates around its shared middle while the whole pair travels down the rut | A stick being carried; a propeller on a post |
| **Top spin + precession** | A disc stands on its flat end, spins at 136 RPM, and its axis wobbles slowly against the tick | A coin settling; a fidget spinner |
| **Jump gap** | A magnet sits still across a visible gap, then crosses it in one frame and slams into its partner | A roll; a cut; something thrown |
| **Rebound ring** | After a slam the pair buzzes and settles over about four eighths | A bounce; a dead stop |
| **Counter-rotating stack** | Alternating discs in a column spin opposite ways at once | The whole column spinning |
| **Standing vibration** | Bars buzz in place hard enough to blur, going nowhere | Sliding; falling over |

Kept from the orbit pass: cyclotron orbit (C), magnetic gears (D), rotating field (G), bound

## The jump gap (how to make it read)

This is the single most likely thing to come back wrong, so it gets its own rules. A neodymium
leap is over in a frame, and a model will happily replace it with a roll or a hard cut.

1. **Show the gap first.** Three to six body-widths of clean, empty dirt or wood, held for at
   least one full beat. If the audience has not seen the gap, there was no jump.
2. **Both bodies dead still before it.** No creep, no roll-in. Stillness is the wind-up.
3. **Cross on one frame.** Motion blur, a streak, nothing in between. Land the impact on the
   **68 BPM kick**, never on an eighth.
4. **Pay the impact.** Grit puff, one skid, and a **rebound ring** over about four eighths as the
   pair buzzes together and settles. The ring is what sells the force.
5. **Never cut inside the jump.** Continuous single take, or it reads as an edit.
6. **One per clip.** Two if the clip is a build. Never a stream of them.

Best homes: **B** (doorway, first jump of the piece, small), **D** (alley, the big one),
**L** (cabinet, the jump that starts the chain).

## Dancer bank (objects that vibrate on the eighths)

More things moving, none of them travelling. Pick three or four per clip, never all of them —
a room where everything shakes reads as an earthquake, not a beat.

**Cabinet (indoor)**
glass marbles hopping in the shelf grooves · wine-glass rims ringing without moving · speaker
cones pumping · a hanging pull-chain ticking against the cabinet side · washers loose on a nail ·
a saucer of water with rings crossing it · ball bearings loose in a tin lid · a key ring on a
hook · lamp filament flickering a hair off the grid · shelf dust jumping in the lamp beam

**Alley (dirt)**
fence slats shivering · frost ticking on the wire · grit and small gravel hopping in the ruts ·
a section of chain-link buzzing like a screen · a loose corrugated sheet oil-canning on the kick ·
a bare bulb swinging a few degrees · nails standing in a board · a hubcap leaning on the fence
ringing · dust motes pulsing in the tower light

**Lawn (unreal green)**
dew beads jumping off the blades · flower heads nodding on the kick · grass tips shivering on the
eighths · coil housings humming visibly · standing water in a footprint with concentric rings ·
a sprinkler head ticking · the too-saturated petals shedding one at a time on the downbeat

**Placement rule.** Indoors, dancers are glass and metal — bright, small, fast. In the alley they
are dry and gritty. On the lawn they are wet. That is how the rooms stay distinguishable when the
magnets are doing the same job in all three.

## Test order for this pass

1. **D jump gap.** Highest risk, highest payoff. If the cross is a roll or a cut, nothing else in
   this pass matters — fix it before generating anything else.
2. **K mutual orbit.** Two equal bodies, empty center. If Imagine puts a big sphere in the middle
   and gives it a moon, kill the take.
3. **A disc stack** counter-rotating. Proves cylinders read as cylinders indoors.
4. **G dumbbell tumble** across the lawn while the coils sequence. Proves rods travel while rotating.
5. Then the dancer layers, room by room — cabinet, alley, lawn — checking that nothing that hops
   has started to travel.
