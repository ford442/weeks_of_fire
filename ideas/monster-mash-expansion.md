## More Amy & Alec beats

1. **The gear check.** Alec silently clips one of Amy's spare carabiners to a passing
   Furbie's cape. The Furbie keeps walking. Neither acknowledges it.
2. **Trail etiquette.** Amy tries to teach the porcelain dolls to dance. They already know.
   They are better than her. She keeps teaching anyway.
3. **Alec's assessment.** He lowers the aviators one inch to look at the melting Stay-Puft,
   raises them again, and says only: "Marshmallow. Soft target."
4. **Headlamp gag.** Every time Amy turns her head the beam whites out whichever monster it
   lands on — a Furbie, Nixon, the Cthulhu tentacle — each recoiling like a vampire.
5. **Spear rack.** Alec plants the sharpened walking stick in the lawn and three bats in
   Hawaiian shirts land on it in a row like a perch.
6. **Gary, finally.** During the outro, one fleece sleeve waves weakly from behind a giant
   pumpkin. Amy doesn't see it. The black-coated spy steps over him without looking down.
7. **Punch bowl.** Amy offers Alec an electrolyte tablet. He drops it in the punch. The
   punch turns neon blue and something in the bowl screams.
8. **The forty-five minutes.** Alec checks his watch mid-dance and mutters "Forty-five
   minutes. Fifty if the mud is thick." Nobody asks what for.

## More werewolf / Thriller beats

1. **Choreography contagion.** Each time he passes a group they involuntarily join the
   Thriller line for four beats, then snap out of it looking embarrassed.
2. **Glove reveal.** Close on the sequined glove catching the red emergency strobe; a
   single sequin falls off in slow motion and lands in the dew.
3. **The mirror.** He moonwalks past the neon-green slime pool and his reflection is a
   perfectly ordinary man in a jacket, walking forwards.
4. **Wolfman rivalry.** The classic 1940s Wolfman at the punch bowl watches the moonwalk,
   attempts it, and falls immediately. Crypt Keeper cackles and drops his femur baton.
5. **Frankenstein counter-move.** The 1930s Frankenstein already in the imagery bank
   moonwalks *toward* him in the opposite direction — a slow, silent, backwards standoff.
6. **Exit.** He moonwalks straight into the fog wall and does not come out. His shadow does.
7. **Outro carryover.** In the B&W Spy vs. Spy sequence he's still gliding in silhouette,
   the only thing in frame with a light source on it.

  in sync, no puppeteer visible.

- **Biscuit (white dog)** wears exactly one Halloween accessory and is deeply unhappy.
- **A neighbor** in normal clothes stands at the property line holding a bowl of candy,
  waiting for someone to come get it. Nobody does. He is there in every wide shot.
- **The Ghostbusters** flee past a second time, still from the same golden retriever.

## Staging & camera ideas

- **One-take pass:** attempt segment 4 as a single 45-second Steadicam walk through the whole
  party instead of rapid cuts — keep both versions, cut between them on the beat.
- **Style-shift on the beat:** hold the frame but change render style every four bars —
  photoreal → VHS → PS1 → inflatable practical → back. Same blocking each time.
- **Headlamp as key light:** let Amy's headlamp be the only motivated light for one shot,
  swinging wildly and lighting whatever it hits.
- **Silence drop:** kill all audio for one beat at the hologram glitch — no music, no
  crowd, just fog — then slam back in on the downbeat.
- **Vertical crop pass:** generate 2B and 4B in 9:16 as well; the moonwalk and the
  carabiner flail both read as standalone social clips.

## Prompt-craft notes for Grok Imagine

- Long prompts hold better when ordered: **subject → wardrobe → blocking → background
  layers → light → lens → grade → style tag**. All segment prompts follow this.
- Grok drops the *last* clause first under load, so keep `Grok Imagine style` and the grade
  words near the end but not alone — repeat one anchor detail (fog, string lights) late.
- Wardrobe detail is what keeps the cameos recognizable across generations. Never
  compress "magenta and safety-orange shell jacket, teal zip-off convertible pants,
  headlamp, carabiners" down to "hiking gear."
- If a generation comes back mushy, cut **background layers** first — never wardrobe,
  never lighting.
- Style clash is intentional. If a batch looks too coherent, add one explicitly wrong
  element (low-poly PS1, inflatable practical) rather than re-rolling the whole prompt.
