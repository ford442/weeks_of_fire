Featuring Rubella, Lillith, and Kenji “Shred” Sato of Glam-Sham-Poo

## Concept

Screeching glam metal energy as a hyper-saturated Japanese TV commercial for an absurd heavy-metal shampoo. Shot as if running on the EyeWash Station TV or blasting from the laundry-room vending machine.

**Glam-Sham-Poo** is both:
- The company / neon region (**Glamora** in **Sham-Poo Prefecture**)
- A famous college (the kind of place where the dorms have better pyrotechnics than the chemistry labs and the student handbook has a whole section on “authorized volume”)

**Product**: Ultra Screech Formula – “Heavy Metal Hair Detox Shampoo”. Turns split ends into power chords. Hair so loud it violates HOA noise ordinances *and* the college noise curfew.

**Cast**:
- **Kenji “Shred” Sato** – Official brand ambassador / alumnus of Glam-Sham-Poo College. Big hair, leather fringe, pure hype.
- **Rubella** – Deadpan endorsement. Exhausted cabaret energy.
- **Lillith** – Chaotic high-energy endorsement. Starts the volume, keeps raising it.

## Product & College Lore

**Ultra Screech Formula** — "Heavy Metal Hair Detox Shampoo." Turns split ends into
power chords. Hair so loud it violates the HOA noise ordinance *and* the college noise
curfew, which are enforced by two agencies that do not acknowledge each other.

**Variants**
- **Ultra Screech** — the flagship. The only one anyone buys.
- **Quiet Formula** — banned. Nobody will say by whom. Funnier than Ultra Screech being legal.
- **Freshman Orientation Size** — sold at move-in, at the table next to the meal plans.

**Banned ingredient**: *liquid feedback*. Written into the regulation by someone who had
clearly just heard a guitar solo. Both the HOA and the chemistry building cite it, for
completely different reasons.

**Glam-Sham-Poo College** — the dorms have better pyrotechnics than the chem labs.
Student handbook **§12: Authorized Volume** runs four pages and has a scorch mark on the
facing page. Alumni claim immunity from HOA citations; the HOA has never agreed to this
and has never successfully argued it either.

**Reactions**: the Pizza Guy watches the commercial on the laundry-room TV and does not
react — bag at chest height, order still correct. He is the control group.

## Production Notes – Stills → Video Pipeline

This is the same workflow we can use for lots of other cutaways:

1. Generate strong Grok Imagine stills (performance, product, environment)
2. Write / generate the audio (spoken or full Minimax track)
3. Assemble as timed slideshow or simple motion (Ken Burns / subtle zooms if needed)
4. **Lipsync strategy** (because post-facto lipsync is hard):
   - Prefer **beat-sync** and visual energy over perfect mouth shapes
   - Keep singers **non-visible or off-camera** for the heavy vocal lines when possible
   - When we do want on-camera vocals, only put the specific lines we care about syncing live (short, punchy, or already rhythmic)
   - Everything else can be treated as performance / attitude / dance under the track

This keeps the cutaways fast to make and still feels musical.

## Why This Doesn't Sound Like a Cosmetic Ad

Real cosmetic brand spots (Maybelline, CoverGirl, L'Oréal) lean on bright dance-pop, clean
polished production, aspirational female vocals, and almost no distortion — the gloss stays
high. A rebellious product line might get a light rock edge, and that is the whole range.

Ultra Screech does the opposite on purpose: real glam-metal guitar language, treated
sincerely, selling shampoo. The commercial polish is a thin layer on top so it still reads
as something a late-night joke network would air at 3 AM without checking.
