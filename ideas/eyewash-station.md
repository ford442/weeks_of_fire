# EyeWash Station — Channel / Network Concept

*Raw concept file. Nothing here is locked. Promote what earns it.*

**The pitch:** every musical cutaway in *Weeks on Fire* is secretly **airing on
a channel**. EyeWash Station is a low-power broadcast affiliate that nobody
subscribes to, nobody cancels, and everybody has apparently seen. It signs on
at unclear hours. It signs off at 3:44. It is on in the background of half the
scenes we've already written.

**Why the name works:** an eyewash station is the thing bolted to the wall for
after you've already been splashed. "Eyewash" is also old slang for pretty
nonsense meant to distract you. Both readings are the show.

**Taglines (pick one, retire the rest):**
- *"Rinse for fifteen minutes. Do not blink."*
- *"You've seen something. We'll take it from here."*
- *"Broadcasting since — well. Broadcasting."*
- *"Channel 12½. Between the ones you meant to watch."*

---

## Why it's useful structurally

- **Frames the cutaways.** "Choose Your Basalt" stops being a random
  educational cabaret number and becomes *EyeWash Station daytime programming*.
  Same for every future one-off.
- **Gives us a container for orphans.** Any 20-second idea too small for an
  episode can air as a bumper, an ident, or a fake ad.
- **Explains recurrence without explaining it.** The Pizza Guy shows up in
  every episode because he's in the station's rotation. Nobody says this.
- **Gives the site a spine.** The Suggestions tab could eventually read as a
  **program schedule** instead of a list.

## Station identity (5–10s idents & bumpers)

These are cheap to generate and enormously tone-setting. Each is one shot.

1. **The Fixture.** Close on an actual wall-mounted eyewash station in a
   fluorescent hallway. The twin nozzles turn slowly toward camera. Logo card.
2. **Rinse Timer.** A 15-minute countdown clock in a tiled room. It ticks down
   to 14:59 and resets. Forever. Bottom-third: *DO NOT BLINK.*
3. **Sign-On.** Bars and tone at dawn, except the tone is four bars of the
   Twilight Time pad and one bar of something that isn't a tone.
4. **The Eye.** Extreme close-up of an eye being rinsed. The reflection in the
   iris is the laser snake battle. The eye is not distressed.
5. **Skeleton S&P.** The Ep3 union skeleton stamps a script APPROVED with a
   rubber stamp. The stamp reads *PROBABLY FINE.*
6. **Biscuit Ident.** Biscuit trots across a blank studio floor with the logo
   in her mouth. Logo exits frame. Card never appears. Show just starts.

> flickering fluorescent hallway, twin chrome nozzles slowly rotating toward
> the camera, institutional green tile, faint steam, 1980s broadcast ident
> aesthetic, slight VHS chroma bleed, cinematic, Grok Imagine style"

## Scene seeds

### "Sign-Off at 3:44"
The station plays the national anthem over stock footage of the cul-de-sac.
The anthem is slightly wrong — a bar too long, a chord that resolves late.
The card comes up: *WE NOW CONCLUDE OUR BROADCAST DAY.* Then it doesn't
conclude. The card sits there. At 3:44 it comes back on with **the same
sign-off**. Someone watching at home says nothing and gets a glass of water.
*Ties to: the 3:44 loop incident, Ep2 nocturnal energy.*

### "Please Stand By"
Full-frame TECHNICAL DIFFICULTIES card. Behind it, clearly audible: a laser
battle, a gavel, a poodle, and someone politely asking whether anyone has a
form for this. Runtime as long as we dare. **The card never cuts away.** The
joke is entirely in the audio and the patience.
*Pure-audio cutaway. Cheapest thing in the series. Possibly the best.*

### "The EyeWash Station Telethon"
A pledge drive for a station that cannot prove it exists. Phone bank staffed
by Karen (taking complaints, not pledges), Dale (gaveling the tote board),
Jackalyn (reading her heart rate on-air as the total), and one phone that
rings only when nobody is near it. The Pizza Guy delivers to the phone bank
and, without breaking stride, answers a line and takes a pledge.
Tote board total goes **down**. Nobody mentions it.

### "Master Control, 4 AM"
Single location, one operator, wall of monitors. Every monitor shows a
different thing we've already made. One monitor shows this room. One monitor
shows this room **from a few seconds ahead**. The operator does not look at
that one — not out of fear, just professional discipline. He fills out a log
sheet. He goes to get coffee. The monitors keep airing.

### "Standards & Practices"
The skeleton reviews a cutaway for broadcast compliance and rules that the
lava is fine, the mirrors are fine, the swapped lives are fine, but the
folding chairs are "a liability." Cuts the chairs. The Two are delighted —
they weren't going to sit anyway.

## Characters (station side)

### The Continuity Voice
**Role:** Between-programs announcer. Never seen. Should never be seen.
**Voice:** Warm, over-rehearsed, institutional. Reads apocalyptic copy in the
cadence of a tire commercial.
**Lines:**
- "Coming up next — *something you'll want to sit down for.* Please sit down."
- "We'll return to your program. We always do."
- "That was a rerun. So is this."
- "The following contains flashing lights, unresolved chords, and one
  character who knows."
**Relationship to Radio Voice (Ep2):** deliberately unclear. Possibly the same
person on a different shift. Never confirm.

### Oz — Overnight Master Control
**Role:** The one human employee we ever see. Runs the board from midnight to
sign-off. Cardigan, thermos, laminated log sheet, absolute calm.
**Personality:** Not brave, not curious — **procedural**. Treats a monitor
showing the future the way you'd treat a flickering bulb: notes it, files it,
does not escalate.
**Sample lines:**
- "It's not haunted, it's *unscheduled*."
- "I don't program it. I just make sure it goes out clean."
- *(to the future monitor)* "Not yet."
**Episode fit:** Anchor for any station-set cutaway. Natural counterpart to
the Pizza Guy — two men who have made peace with the impossible via paperwork.

### Marguerite Flood — Station Manager
**Role:** Owns the license. Or says she does. Has never produced the license.
**Look:** Business formal from a decade that keeps sliding. Reading glasses on
a chain. One earring that is definitely a transmitter.
**Personality:** Speaks in FCC-adjacent legalese about supernatural events.
Refers to viewers as "the affected."
**Sample lines:**
- "We are not liable for what you saw. We are liable for the *aspect ratio*."
- "The tower predates the town. That's a zoning matter, not a mystery."
- "Everyone gets fifteen minutes. That's not fame, that's the rinse duration."

### The S&P Skeleton *(promoted from Ep3)*
Same union skeleton, new day job. Stamps scripts *PROBABLY FINE.* Still on a
strict break schedule. His notes are always about furniture, never about the
cosmic horror.

## Fake sponsors & ad breaks

Fifteen-second slots. Play them completely straight.

- **PERPETUAL OPTICS** — "Because you're going to keep looking."
- **QUARTER TO SEVENTY-FIVE FINANCIAL** — "Retirement planning for people who
  suspect time is not linear." (Small print: *not a bank.*)
- **BRAD'S BINS** — "Every shade of blue. One of them is correct."
- **NAPKIN CORP** — a single napkin on a table for fifteen seconds. Logo. Out.
- **THE VALE SISTERS' HOME COLLECTION** — "Mood lighting. Some assembly. Some
  fire."
- **PUBLIC SERVICE ANNOUNCEMENT** — "If you see a purple light in your
  driveway, that's a decorative accent. Do not report it. Enjoy it."

## Open questions

- Is EyeWash Station **diegetic** (characters watch it) or a **framing
  device** (only we see it), or does it slide between? Recommendation: slide,
  never comment.
- Does the station have a **transmitter tower** we can shoot? The spooky
  telephone poles material wants to connect to this. Strongly recommend yes.
- Does the series eventually air **an episode of itself** on the station? Save
  it. That's a finale move, not a season-one move.
