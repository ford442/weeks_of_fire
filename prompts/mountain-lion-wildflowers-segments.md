**Imagine rules:** POV leash unless a segment is marked flower-only. Lion stays deadpan. Flowers may go unreal; the cat does not mug. No attack, no horror, no metal-guitar energy. Replace native clip audio with the two beds.

## The ocean act (2026-09-05)

The pull no longer runs flowers → clearing. It runs **flowers → ocean → flowers again → clearing**.

The single best thing about this is that **the ocean is the crossfade**. `Come Further` runs out
as the field thins into water; the 8–12s fade happens over open sea; `Here They Are` starts as
the blooms come back on the far side. No third bed, no new song to generate — the water sits
exactly where the two takes were already going to overlap.

Structurally the flowers are the corridor and the sea is the doorway. Whatever the cat wanted to
show us is on the other side of it, and the cat knew that the whole time.

## Visual arc

Insistent look-back → oversized lupine pull → painted botanical architecture → the field
becomes the sea → the crossing → far shore, flowers return **wrong and better** → multi-lion
sunlit clearing → gratifying hold.

## Timing

Target **~2:20**. Do not generate a 3-minute wander. Drag A–C ≈ 52s, ocean D–E ≈ 38s across the
crossfade, field F–H ≈ 50s. If you need the old ~2:15 lock back, shorten C and F — never the
crossing, which is the whole point of the pass.

## How the water behaves

Locked, because this is where the idea can go wrong:

- **No swimming, no drowning, no peril.** The cat does not drag anyone under. The tone stays
  gratifying.
- **The flowers become the water rather than stopping at it.** Petals thin into foam; the last
  lupines stand in shallow surf; a tideline of blossom and sea-foam reads as one substance.
- **We cross on something that should not hold** — a causeway of packed floating blooms, a
  sandbar wearing flowers, a kelp path. It holds. Nobody comments.
- **The lion is not impressed by the ocean.** Same walk. Same deadpan. Wet paws at most.
- **The far shore is not a return to the same field.** The flowers come back larger, stranger,
  more painted. Crossing changed the vocabulary.

## Drag corridors (swap bank)

The structure is **corridor → doorway → corridor → arrival**. Flowers/ocean/flowers is the
locked pass, but the corridor slot is reusable — this is how the same cutaway gets re-cut later
without re-boarding it. Each of these is a place the cat could pull us through instead of, or on
the way to, the field. Keep the leash, keep the deadpan, keep it under 20 seconds.

1. **Tall grass at dusk** — seed heads at eye height, everything gold and dry, insects lit like
   sparks. The cheapest corridor and the one most likely to hold up.
2. **A parking structure with the flowers already winning** — cracked concrete, lupines through
   the expansion joints, sodium light. The one corridor that touches the building world.
3. **Kelp forest at the surface line** — half above water, half below, sun in shafts. Natural
   sibling to the crossing; good if the causeway generation fails.
4. **A wheat field mid-wind** — one substance, one color, moving in sheets. Good contrast right
   before the ocean because it is the least surreal thing in the piece.
5. **Fog with shapes in it** — nothing resolves, the leash disappears into white, the cat is a
   suggestion. Use once, briefly, and never twice.
6. **A dry riverbed of flat stones** — the only corridor with no plants; makes the return of the
   flowers on the far shore hit harder.
7. **Under a pier at low tide** — barnacled columns as architecture, water light on the
   undersides, blossoms caught in the pilings. The most literal flowers-meet-ocean handshake.
8. **Snow with flowers coming up through it** — wrong season, no explanation, the cat does not
   care. Save this one; it is a different cutaway waiting to happen.

Reusable corridor prompt skeleton:

```
Cinematic photoreal POV behind a mountain lion on a thin leash pulling us through <CORRIDOR>, the cat deadpan and focused straight ahead, leash taut toward camera, motion blur at the frame edges, <LIGHT>, short-film still, Weeks on Fire visual style, Grok Imagine style
```

## Alternate destinations (totally other ideas)

If the multi-lion clearing ever stops being the payoff, these are the other things the cat could
have been insisting on. One per cut — the joke dies if the destination is a list.

- **The other lions, but they are waiting on us.** Same clearing, except every cat is already
  facing the direction we arrive from. They have been waiting a while. Nothing else changes.
- **One specific flower.** The whole drag — the field, the ocean, the far shore — was to show us
  a single ordinary daisy, correctly sized, growing alone. The cat sits down next to it.
- **The edge of the painted part.** We arrive at a seam where the photoreal field ends and the
  watercolor one begins, and it is a physical edge you can stand at. The cat crosses it. We are
  still holding the leash.
- **The ocean itself was the destination** and the far-shore flowers are a consolation. Ends on
  the cat sitting at the waterline, looking out, done pulling. Quieter, sadder, very good.
- **Where the light comes from.** Follow the golden light to its source and it is just more
  field, brighter. No object, no reveal. The cat is satisfied. We do not get an explanation.

## Quiet humor (ocean act)

- The lion shakes off exactly once, at the worst possible moment for the camera.
- A single blossom rides the water alongside us for the whole crossing and beaches itself first.
- The leash floats. Nobody adjusts for this.
- On the far shore the cat scratches at wet sand as if to bury something, gets bored, walks on.
- Optional, once: a gull-sized thing passes overhead that is clearly made of petals. No reaction
  from anyone, including the gull.
