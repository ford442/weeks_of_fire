## Full dialog (clean copy)

**MARA:** Okay. Let's talk about the pacing in episode two. The transition from the Comet Lounge to the morning sequence. Right now it feels like we leave Ned on the karaoke stage and wake up in a commercial without earning the cut.

**RUBELLA:** We can talk about pacing after we talk about what I put in my mouth for seven hours. Can we talk about this cereal shoot first? The aesthetic is killer — the Lamborghini, the black lace, the gothic estate — but I had to eat seventeen bowls of "Weeks on Fire" cereal for the camera. It tasted like cinnamon and battery acid. If we are doing another take of this, I need real food on set. Not prop cereal. Not "lore-accurate crunch." Food. Preferably something that was a plant in a previous life.

**LILLITH:** Brand consistency.

**MARA:** It's surreal corporate branding. The world sells the apocalypse in breakfast form. That's the joke and the threat.

**RUBELLA:** The joke is my stomach. The threat is me walking.

**LILLITH:** It establishes the surreal corporate branding of the world, Ruby. It works. But my concern is the pacing right after. Look at the safehouse location... This coffee bar. The staff here... they look too normal. We have these tense, moody baristas, but there's no underlying threat. We need to establish that they are the informants. It's too quiet right now. Quiet reads as empty, not coded.

**MARA:** You want menace in the pour-over.

**LILLITH:** I want a network. Eye contact that lasts one beat too long. A ticket stub that isn't a ticket. Someone says "regular" and means "you're being watched."

**MARA:** Right. Good note. We add dialogue there. A coded exchange about the upcoming HOA Meeting. Not the meeting itself — just the weather system before the storm. And for the soundtrack under that scene, we keep it tense. A synth bassline. Something driving. Played out on that custom one-octave chromatic layout we mapped out. It'll keep the audience completely on edge without overpowering the dialogue. The crunch stays diegetic. The bass is the conspiracy. Episode three bridges the gap — ramps the tension, sharpens you two against each other and the lore. Episode four is the HOA Meeting. That's where the informants cash in and the branding stops being cute.

**RUBELLA:** Fine. But next cereal take: oatmeal. Human oatmeal.

**LILLITH:** Brand it "Weeks on Fire Overnight Oats" and she'll do twelve bowls.

**RUBELLA:** Eight. Tops.

**MARA:** I'll put it in the draft.

**CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O.):** We'll return to your program. We always do.
