# The Long Way Up

*Long-form dialog scene — Episode 4 suggestion. The wordy episode. Meeting Riley, and losing the entire rest of the day to a mountain.*

**Slugline:** EXT. RIDGE ROAD / FIRE ROAD / KESTREL RIDGE — 2:10 PM THROUGH NIGHTFALL
**Runtime target:** 9–12 min (≈11 script pages) — **the longest dialog block in the series, on purpose**
**Music cue:** **"The Honest Height"** — original Minimax track, held out entirely until Beat H. No underscore before nightfall. The bed until then is wind, gravel, one loose fan belt, and cicadas that stop when the sun does.
**Status:** draft — scene suggestion for Episode 4

**Cast:** RUBELLA VALE (driving) · LILLITH VALE (passenger, prosecuting) · RILEY (new) · CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O., car radio, twice)

**Spine:** Two women who normally say eleven words an episode pick up a stranger at two in the afternoon and talk her up a mountain until dark. Nothing happens. Everything is discussed.

---

## The Joke (read this before writing a single line)

*Weeks on Fire* is a nearly silent, extremely silly show. This scene is what happens
when somebody sits on the remote and the channel lands on a **mid-season cable
procedural that then turns into a chatty sci-fi movie**, and nobody in the cast
notices or objects. They just start talking in that register and keep talking for
eleven minutes.

The comedy is **volume, structure, and commitment**, not jokes-per-page:

- **Act breaks.** The scene is formatted with a TEASER and three ACTS, like a 44-minute network drama, for a sequence in which three people drive up a hill and then walk up the rest of it.
- **Wrong-genre grammar.** The meeting is played as a traffic stop. The gate is played as a warrant discussion. The fire road is played as an exposition act with a whiteboard energy and no whiteboard.
- **Over-specification.** Nobody says "the tower." They say the decommissioned Kestrel Ridge weather repeater, and then they say its decommissioning year, and then they argue about the year.
- **Riley is not mystical.** She is *correct*. That is the funniest available choice and the one the earlier draft missed.
- **The clock is a character.** Timestamps are on screen. It is 2:10 PM in the teaser and 9:40 PM in the tag, and the audience is meant to feel every hour of that.

**What this scene is not:** a daytime drama. There are no meaningful looks held for
four seconds, no confessions about the past, no one saying "I've never told anyone
this." If a line could be delivered by two people on a soap-opera patio holding
mugs, cut it. Every sincere line must be immediately load-bearing on something
practical — a distance, a padlock, a frequency, a boot.

**Emotional notes:** Warmth arrives through logistics, not disclosure. These three
become friends because they solved a coolant problem together, not because they
shared anything. The first genuine silence in the whole scene lands at Beat G and
is written, timed, and non-negotiable.

**Hard rules:**
- **Never confirm what the antenna is.** It is a dead weather repeater. It may also be the EyeWash Station transmitter. Riley has a theory. Nobody rules on it. (See `ideas/eyewash-station.md`, `ideas/backlog.md`.)
- **Riley is never wrong about a fact and never smug about it.**
- **Rubella's pseudoscience lecture gets corrected.** This is the first time in the series that happens. Play it as a small tragedy.
- **The Vales do not change clothes.** They climb a mountain in black lace. Wardrobe does not rescue them; the scene does not comment on it more than twice.
- **No copyrighted lyrics.** `[Section]` placeholder tags only.

## TEASER

### A — 2:10 PM — The Traffic Stop That Isn't One

```
EXT. RIDGE ROAD — DAY

A two-lane county road with no shoulder and no reason. Dry gold hills.
Heat coming off the asphalt in sheets.

A CARD, in the flat institutional font of a station bumper:

        THE FOLLOWING PROGRAM CONTAINS DIALOGUE.

The card holds two full seconds. It does not explain itself.

A dusty sedan comes around the bend at twenty miles an hour. RUBELLA
VALE drives. LILLITH VALE has her arm and most of her upper body out
the passenger window. Both are in black lace. Both are dressed for a
funeral in a city ninety miles from here.

Ahead of them, walking the center line with a forty-liter pack and the
posture of someone doing math, is RILEY. Sun-bleached. Green halter.
An amethyst pendant catching the light like it's been told to.

The sedan slows to her walking pace and stays there.
```

```
LILLITH
Ma'am.

(Riley keeps walking.)

LILLITH (CONT'D)
Ma'am. Do you know why we stopped you?

RILEY
You didn't stop me. I'm walking. You decelerated. Those are two
different events with two different paperwork trails.

(Beat. Lillith turns her head very slightly toward Rubella
without breaking eye contact with Riley.)

LILLITH
(quiet, to Rubella)
She's good.

RUBELLA
(leaning across, not slowing)
Ma'am, are you aware that this road is used by exactly four vehicles
a day, that two of them are the same truck, and that the mountain
lions clock in at approximately six-fifteen?

RILEY
Six-forty. They're seasonal. And the truck's a water hauler for
the pistachio operation on the north side, so it's the same truck
twice, which makes it three vehicles a day, which makes this the
safest road in the county on a per-capita basis.

(Long beat. The sedan does not accelerate.)

LILLITH
Rubella.

RUBELLA
I heard it.
```

## ACT ONE

### B — 2:40 PM — Aggressive Small Talk (The Interview)

```
INT. SEDAN — MOVING — DAY

Riley in the back, pack on the seat beside her like a passenger. The
cooler is on the floor. Lillith has turned around fully, one knee up
on the seat, in the position of a detective who has decided to be
casual about it.
```

```
LILLITH
Okay. Weather antenna. Is that a hobby, a job, or a personality
disorder?

RILEY
It's a hobby that used to be a job.

LILLITH
Which job.

RILEY
I did site surveys. Nine years. You drive to a piece of hardware
nobody has thought about since the Clinton administration and you
write down whether it's still there.

LILLITH
And?

RILEY
It's almost always still there. That's the finding. Nine years of
findings and the finding is: nobody takes anything down. Taking it
down costs money. Leaving it up costs nothing. So the country is
just — accumulating.

RUBELLA
(eyes on the road)
That's the most depressing sentence anyone has said in this car.

LILLITH
It's two-forty in the afternoon.

RUBELLA
I'm aware of the time, Lillith.
```

```
LILLITH
Follow-up. You were walking. On a road with three vehicles a day.
Twelve miles from the ridge.

RILEY
Eleven point four.

LILLITH
Twelve miles from the ridge, with a pack, in August, at two in the
afternoon, which is the worst hour of the worst month —

RILEY
I know what month it is.

LILLITH
— so either your car is somewhere behind us in a condition you'd
rather not describe, or you don't have one.

(Beat.)

RILEY
The car's in Tulare.

LILLITH
Is it running?

RILEY
It's in Tulare.

(Lillith turns back around, satisfied, and faces front.)

LILLITH
(to the windshield)
She's got a whole thing.

RUBELLA
Everyone's got a whole thing.

LILLITH
We don't.

RUBELLA
We have the biggest thing anyone in this car has ever had.
```

### C — 3:25 PM — The Gate

```
EXT. FIRE ROAD GATE — DAY

The sedan stops at a steel swing gate across a graded dirt road. A
sign, faded, official, and extremely wordy:

    SEASONAL CLOSURE — FOREST ROAD 4N22 — VEHICLE ACCESS PROHIBITED
    MAY 15 – NOV 1 PER ORDINANCE 12-114(b) — VIOLATORS SUBJECT TO
    CITATION — FOOT AND EQUESTRIAN TRAFFIC PERMITTED

A padlock hangs on the chain. All three stand in front of the gate in
a row, reading, like a jury.
```

```
LILLITH
"Per ordinance twelve dash one fourteen, paren b."

RUBELLA
There's a paren b.

LILLITH
There is always a paren b.

RUBELLA
What's in paren a?

RILEY
Definitions.

LILLITH
(rounding on her)
How do you know what's in paren a.

RILEY
Because paren a is always definitions.

(Beat. Lillith accepts this. It is correct.)
```

```
LILLITH
(examining the padlock, not touching it)
This lock has been cut.

RILEY
Yes.

LILLITH
And re-hung. Somebody cut this lock and then hung it back up so it
would read as locked from a moving vehicle.

RILEY
Yes.

LILLITH
Riley.

RILEY
In March.

LILLITH
That's a felony.

RILEY
It's a citation. Paren b.

(Rubella has already opened the gate and gotten back in the car.)

RUBELLA
(through the window)
Are we litigating or are we driving.
```

## ACT TWO

### D — 4:05 PM — The Exposition Act

```
INT. SEDAN — FIRE ROAD — CLIMBING — DAY

Washboard dirt. The car complains. The angle of the road is now
visible in the frame. Riley has moved forward, elbows on the seat
backs, between them. This is the sci-fi movie now and everyone has
agreed to it without discussion.

Rubella turns the radio on. Static with something in it.
```

```
CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O.)
(from the radio, warm, far away)
— and we'll return to your program. We always do.

(Static. Rubella turns it back off. Nobody comments.)
```

```
RUBELLA
Explain the tower.

RILEY
Which part.

RUBELLA
The part where a woman walks eleven miles to a dead antenna.

RILEY
Eleven point four. Okay. The repeater's a hundred and ninety feet on
a ridge that's already at fifty-two hundred, so the effective height
is what matters, not the structure. That's called the honest height.
It's the only height that isn't a real estate term.

LILLITH
(genuinely) That's a good phrase.

RILEY
It's not mine. And it's dead — no power, no feed line, the cabinet
at the base has been open to weather since before I was doing this.
But the geometry doesn't die. The ridge still does the thing the
ridge does. On a summer night when the valley air stacks up warm
over cool, signals stop going up and start going sideways. They ride
the layer. You can sit under a dead antenna in the middle of nowhere
and hear a station eleven hundred miles away like it's across the
street. That's ducting. It's not spooky, it's just Tuesday.

(Beat.)

RUBELLA
It's Thursday.

RILEY
It's ducting on Thursdays too.
```

```
LILLITH
(carefully, the way you set a trap)
Rubella has a background in this.

RUBELLA
I do.

RILEY
Great.

RUBELLA
(settling into the lecture posture; the series
register, dead-level, clinical)
Most people don't know that a utility structure of sufficient height
generates a standing ground current in the surrounding soil, which
is why the rats congregate, which is why the hydrogen sulfide —

RILEY
That's not — sorry. Sorry. That's not a thing.

(The car goes quiet. Lillith's head comes around slowly.)

RUBELLA
Excuse me?

RILEY
Ground current's real. It's a step-potential issue at the base of
energized structures. But that one's been dead for twenty-eight
years, so there's no current, so there's no rats, so there's no —
(gesturing, kindly)
— the whole rest of it.

(A silence of a very specific quality. Lillith is delighted and
hiding it at approximately forty percent efficiency.)

RUBELLA
(after a long moment, evenly)
I've been saying that for three years.

RILEY
I know. I've heard you say it.

LILLITH
(immediately)
Where.

RILEY
(beat; to the window)
Around.
```

### E — 5:20 PM — The Car Stops Being Part of the Plan

```
EXT. FIRE ROAD — TURNOUT — LATE AFTERNOON

The sedan is stopped at an angle. Hood up. Steam, or the memory of
steam. Riley is under the hood to the shoulders. Lillith holds a
phone flashlight in daylight, for no reason, with total commitment.
Rubella sits on the cooler.
```

```
RILEY
(from under the hood)
It's not the head gasket.

LILLITH
Nobody said head gasket.

RILEY
Somebody's about to say head gasket. It's a hose clamp, it's forty
years old, it did the thing forty-year-old hose clamps do on an
eleven percent grade in August. If we let it cool for an hour and
fill it, it will get down. It will not get up.

RUBELLA
Define "up."

RILEY
Two point one miles of switchbacks with an average grade of
eleven and a maximum of eighteen.

LILLITH
And "down"?

RILEY
Down is gravity and a prayer, and you'll both be fine, and you
should probably do that.

(Beat. Lillith turns the flashlight off.)

LILLITH
How long on foot.

RILEY
Two and a half hours at your pace.

LILLITH
You've assessed my pace.

RILEY
I assessed your shoes at a hundred yards, ma'am.
```

```
(Riley comes out from under the hood, opens her pack, and
produces, with no ceremony, one pair of trail boots.)

RILEY (CONT'D)
Sevens.

RUBELLA
I'm an eight.

LILLITH
I'm a seven.

RUBELLA
You're a seven and a half and you have been lying about it since
high school.

LILLITH
(taking the boots)
And I've never been happier about it.

(Lillith sits on the bumper, removes one heel, puts on one
boot, and stands. She looks down at herself: one boot, one
stiletto. She takes two experimental steps on the grade.
It works. It works badly, but it works.)

LILLITH (CONT'D)
(to Rubella, holding out the second boot)
Even split.

RUBELLA
(taking it; already removing a heel)
This is the most functional thing we've ever done.
```

## ACT THREE

### F — 6:45 PM — The Switchbacks (Overlap)

```
EXT. SWITCHBACKS — GOLDEN HOUR

The climb. Long lens: three small figures on a dirt scar cut across a
gold hillside, the valley opening up behind them, the light going
long and orange and specific.

They are strung out over forty yards. This entire beat is shot wide
and cut close and the dialog OVERLAPS — three people who have been
talking for four and a half hours and have stopped waiting for turns.
```

```
LILLITH
— no, my point, my point is that if the layer does the thing, if
the air stacks up, you're not hearing the past, you're hearing the
present from somewhere rude —

RILEY
Right —

LILLITH
— which is worse, actually, which is much worse, because the past
is finished and Denver is ongoing —

RUBELLA
(twenty yards back, not raising her voice)
It's not Denver.

RILEY
It's usually Salt Lake.

LILLITH
(without turning)
Why is it Salt Lake.

RILEY
Geometry.

LILLITH
That's not an answer, that's the shape of an answer —

RILEY
It's a mountain and a lake and a lot of watts.

LILLITH
Okay, that's an answer.
```

```
(A stretch of just breathing and gravel. The grade is doing
its work. Rubella has closed the gap.)

RUBELLA
(to Riley, conversational, not confessional)
This is the most talking I've done in a car since 2019.

RILEY
We're not in the car.

RUBELLA
I'm counting the car.

RILEY
Then it's the most talking you've done since 2019.

LILLITH
(ahead, not turning around)
She's counting the car!

RUBELLA
(calling up)
I'm counting the car!
```

```

### H — 9:40 PM — The Fire, and the Song

```

RILEY

LILLITH

RILEY

RUBELLA
It's got a hose clamp.

RILEY
(writing)
It's got a hose clamp.

It's dead. The ridge isn't.

excellent acoustics.

LILLITH
That's the first thing you've said all day I can't fact-check.

RILEY
(starting to sing, quietly, without announcing it)
Mm.

## Production Notes

**Runtime target:** 9–12 min. This is intentionally the longest continuous dialog block in *Weeks on Fire* and should be the only one of its kind in the series. It works because every other episode is nearly silent.

**Time-of-day discipline (the "rest of the day" requirement):**

| Beat | Clock | Location | Light |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 2:10 PM | Ridge road | Flat, brutal, overhead |
| B | 2:40 PM | Sedan, moving | Hot, hazy, high sun |
| C | 3:25 PM | Fire road gate | Hard shadows, dust |
| D | 4:05 PM | Sedan, climbing | Sun starting to angle |
| E | 5:20 PM | Turnout, hood up | Warm, long shadows |
| *insert* | *5:40–6:25 PM* | *Cut-through woods → Pulpit Overlook* | *Deep shade → gold cliff* — see `no-other-human-sounds.md` |
| F | 6:45 PM | Switchbacks (from the overlook side) | Full golden hour |
| G | 8:05 PM | Saddle | Dusk / blue hour |
| H | 9:40 PM | Antenna base | Full dark, firelight, stars |

Each beat must be visibly a different hour. If two consecutive beats could be
the same time of day, the scene has failed its one structural job.

**Music sync summary:**
- Beats A–G: **no score at all.** Wind, gravel, engine, cicadas, one loose belt.
- Beat D: Continuity Voice from the radio, four seconds, then off.
- Beat H: "The Honest Height," diegetic → past-diegetic, never large.
- Beat H tail: Continuity Voice from an impossible direction, under the song.
