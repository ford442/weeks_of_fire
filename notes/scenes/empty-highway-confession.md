# Empty Highway Confession

*Long-form dialog scene — Episode 2 expansion. Stretch deliverable; a driver, a radio, and nobody else.*

**Slugline:** INT./EXT. INTERSTATE — MOVING CAR — 4:17 AM
**Runtime target:** 3–4 min (≈3½ script pages)
**Music cue:** **"Whatever Lets Us Be"** or **"Les Ondes Courtes"** — enters diegetically from the radio at Beat D, sung along with quietly, then fully. Bed underneath throughout: shortwave hiss, tire drone, the tick of a turn signal nobody has cancelled.
**Status:** draft
**Dialog versions (alts):** [`versions/empty-highway-confession-versions.md`](versions/empty-highway-confession-versions.md)


**Cast:** THE DRIVER (alone) · RADIO VOICE (V.O. only, never seen, never confirmed)

**Spine:** The confession starts practical and ends honest. The road is the confessional; the radio is a priest who may not be there.

**Emotional notes:** The driver is not in crisis. That's the trap of this scene — play it as a man or woman doing a long, boring, ordinary thing and accidentally telling the truth because the truth is what's left when the podcast ends. No tears. One laugh, in the wrong place.

**Hard rule:** Never confirm whether the Radio Voice is a broadcast, the Continuity Voice on a different shift, or the driver talking to themselves. The Voice may answer a question. It may not answer the same question twice.

### A — 4:17

```
EXT. INTERSTATE — NIGHT

A single pair of taillights on four empty lanes. Landscape on both
sides that could be anything and is nothing.

INT. CAR — CONTINUOUS

THE DRIVER, lit green from below by the instrument panel. One hand on
the wheel at six o'clock, the posture of hour three.

The clock on the dash reads 4:17.

The radio is between stations. Not silent — hissing, with something
in the hiss that keeps almost being music.
```

```
DRIVER
(to nobody)
Okay.
(beat)
Okay.

(He thumbs the tuner. Hiss. Hiss. A fragment of a
string section. Hiss.)

DRIVER (CONT'D)
I should pull over.

(He does not pull over.)

DRIVER (CONT'D)
There's one at ninety-one. There's one at ninety-one with the
lights on, and I'll get a coffee I don't want, and I'll stand
next to the car for four minutes looking at the sky like
somebody in an advertisement, and then I'll get back in.
(beat)
So. No. We're not doing ninety-one.
```

### B — The Practical Confession

```
INT. CAR — CONTINUOUS

The hiss resolves, briefly, into a voice. Warm. Crackly. Half-heard,
the way a station sounds when it is either very far away or has never
been anywhere.
```

```
RADIO VOICE (V.O.)
—still out there. That's the hard part.

DRIVER
(to the radio, mildly)
Yeah, well.

(He waits. Nothing. He talks anyway.)

DRIVER (CONT'D)
I told her I was driving up to see about the house. Which is
true. There is a house. It's got my name on a piece of paper
in a drawer in a building I've never been inside.
(beat)
And I'm going to get there at nine, and I'm going to walk
around it, and I'm going to say "yep" out loud in every room,
and I'm going to drive home. That's the trip. That's the
whole itinerary.

(beat)

Eleven hours for four "yeps."

RADIO VOICE (V.O.)
(static; a fragment)
—quarter to seventy-five and—

DRIVER
(a short laugh, genuinely amused, at nothing)
Sure.
```

### C — The Real One

```
INT. CAR — CONTINUOUS

Headlights of an oncoming truck sweep the interior — one bright pass,
then dark again. He is different in the dark after it.

EXT. HIGHWAY — INTERCUT

A single lit window in the middle of a field with no house around it.
Gone before it can be examined.
```

```
DRIVER
I'm going to say the thing now, because there's nobody in the
car, and because in about ninety miles it gets light and I
won't be able to.

(beat)

I told myself I was just driving to clear my head. Turns out
my head had a whole itinerary.

(beat)

I'm not going up for the house. I'm going up because if I'm
in the car, I'm not deciding. Eleven hours where the correct
thing to do is *keep going straight*. Do you know how rare
that is? Somebody built four lanes and a whole system of
signs to tell me I'm doing it right. Nobody's built anything
like that for the rest of my life.

(beat)

She asked me a question in March. Not a big one. She asked it
kindly. She's asked it twice since, kindly. And every time,
I have said "let me think about it," and then I have gotten
in the car.

(beat)

That's five months of highway for one sentence I could say in
four words.

(beat)

I'm not going to say the four words out loud in the car,
because if I say them in the car they're a rehearsal, and if
they're a rehearsal I'll perform them, and she'll know.

RADIO VOICE (V.O.)
(clear, suddenly, no static at all)
Next song's for everyone who didn't pull over.

DRIVER
(after a beat, quietly)
...Right.
```

### D — The Song

```
INT. CAR — CONTINUOUS

The station arrives. Fully, warmly, as if it had been there the whole
time and the hiss was a courtesy.

The song is one of ours. He knows it. He should not know it.

He sings along under his breath. Badly, and only on the ends of lines
at first — the way people sing when they think they're alone, which
he is, which is the point.

By the second chorus he is singing it properly, both hands on the
wheel, chin up, no self-consciousness left at four-seventeen in the
morning.

No dialogue. Ninety seconds. Let him have it.
```

**Lyric handling:** diegetic Minimax track only — **no copyrighted lyrics, no re-used Ep1 lines**. Use `songs/Whatever_Lets_Us_Be.md` or `songs/Les_Ondes_Courtes.md` as recorded; if a bespoke cue is written, tag structure as `[Verse] [Chorus] [Verse] [Chorus] [Outro — cuts on the billboard]`.

### E — Keep Going

```
EXT. HIGHWAY — PRE-DAWN

The sky at the edge is doing the thing it does at 5 AM where it
admits it was never really black.

The car passes under an enormous illuminated billboard.

The billboard says, in friendly letters, with no logo, no phone
number, and no advertiser:

                        KEEP GOING

It is lit from the top like it is proud of itself.
```

```
INT. CAR — CONTINUOUS

The Driver sees it. Does not slow down. Does not speed up.
```

```
DRIVER
(to the billboard, to the radio, to the four words
he did not say)
Working on it.
```

```
The song cuts off mid-phrase — not faded, cut, the way a signal goes
when you drive out of range of something that was never on a map.

Hiss.

The clock on the dash reads 4:17.

He looks at it for a long moment. Then he watches the road again.
```

```
RADIO VOICE (V.O.)
(very small, under the hiss)
You're still here.
(beat)
That's the hard part.
```

```
Hold on the taillights getting smaller. Let the hiss go long.
```
