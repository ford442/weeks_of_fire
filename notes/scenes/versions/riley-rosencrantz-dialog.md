## RR-A — The Probability of a Truck

**Placement:** Beat A, center line. After deceleration, before get-in.  
**Pitch:** Rosencrantz coin-flip energy, but the coin is a water hauler.

```
RUBELLA
If a truck comes around the bend — and I am not saying it will,
I am saying if — what are the odds it is the same truck twice.

RILEY
One hundred percent, twice, if you mean Darren's hauler. He does
the north pistachio run and comes back empty. That's two passes.
The third vehicle is usually a county maintenance unit that hasn't
been dispatched since April.

RUBELLA
So three vehicles.

RILEY
Three vehicles a day. Safest road in the county on a per-capita
basis, which is a joke that only works if you count the lions.

RUBELLA
Six-fifteen.

RILEY
Six-forty. Seasonal. They're not on the clock. They're on the
ridge because the ridge is there and so are they.

RUBELLA
What if we flipped a coin for each vehicle. Heads it passes.
Tails it doesn't.

RILEY
Then you'd be wrong about how roads work. Vehicles don't consult
coins. They consult schedules, or Darren consults his wife about
whether June needs to be at preschool, and then the truck exists.

RUBELLA
You're saying the truck is inevitable.

RILEY
I'm saying the truck is Tuesday. Inevitable is a word people use
when they didn't check the logbook.

RUBELLA
And us. Are we inevitable.

RILEY
You decelerated. That was a choice. I'm walking. That was a
cheaper choice. The sedan is a third thing that happened because
you got curious, and curiosity is not on any schedule I was given.

RUBELLA
(loud, to the car)
She says we're curiosity!

LILLITH (O.S.)
We prefer "anomaly." Get in before the anomaly walks out of frame.
```

## RR-B — Whether We Are Climbing

**Placement:** Beat D–E, sedan climbing. Ducting lecture interrupted by metaphysics.  
**Pitch:** Are we going up, or is down leaving us?

```
LILLITH
Explain tropospheric ducting again. Slower. Like I am a jury that
hates science but respects confidence.

RILEY
Warm air over cool air. The atmosphere lies about distance. Radio
waves skip farther than they should. A dead repeater can look alive
on a scanner. A living one can look dead. The tower doesn't care.
It's a piece of metal with a funding vote attached.

LILLITH
And we're driving toward it because.

RILEY
Because the ducting window is this week. Next week the lie changes.
You wanted the lecture. That's the lecture.

LILLITH
No — why are we driving toward it. Us. Three women and a hill.

RILEY
You asked where I was headed. I answered. You kept going the same
direction. That's not destiny. That's not following me. That's
coincidence with upholstery.

LILLITH
Rubella thinks it's fate.

RILEY
Rubella thinks in lace. Lace is not a navigational system.

LILLITH
Are we climbing, or is the road falling away beneath us.

RILEY
Eleven percent grade. The road is fixed. The sedan is losing. Those
are measurements. If you want philosophy, the engine is converting
gasoline into heat and regret at a ratio you can hear.

LILLITH
That's poetry.

RILEY
That's a coolant leak. Poetry would be if it fixed itself because
you believed in it.

LILLITH
Do you believe in it.

RILEY
I believe in hose clamps. I have one left. Belief is what you do
when the part is in Fresno until Wednesday.

LILLITH
(long beat)
When we get to the top — if we get to the top — will something
have been waiting for us.

RILEY
Yes. A decommissioned lattice structure and a view. They've been
waiting since ninety-eight. Nobody takes anything down. Waiting is
the default state of hardware in this country.

LILLITH
That's bleak.

RILEY
It's accurate. You don't have to like it.
```

## RR-C — Waiting for the Repeater

**Placement:** No Other Human Sounds — mid-woods, before rally locks in.  
**Pitch:** What are we walking toward? Hamlet never arrives; the repeater might not either.

```
RUBELLA
What is a repeater waiting for.

RILEY
Nothing. It's not waiting. It's standing. Waiting implies hope.
Steel doesn't hope. It rusts at a known rate.

RUBELLA
Then why walk to it.

RILEY
Because I said I would in 2019 and the ducting window is this week
and my car is in Tulare and walking is how I keep appointments with
myself when the county doesn't pay me anymore.

RUBELLA
That's sad.

RILEY
Sad is not a filing system. Date, wind direction, elevation. That's
a filing system. The tower is filed under: still there.

RUBELLA
We could turn around.

RILEY
We could. The road behind us is also a road. Turning around is a
plot choice. I'm not the writer.

RUBELLA
You think there's a writer.

RILEY
I think there's a grade. Grades don't care who wrote them. They
care about your calves.

RUBELLA
The tower — when we get there — will it speak.

RILEY
It will creak. If the wind comes back. Wind is not speech. People
keep confusing the two.

RUBELLA
And if it doesn't creak.

RILEY
Then you'll have climbed a hill to stand next to a thing that
doesn't perform on cue. That's most relationships. You're in lace.
You probably know.

RUBELLA
That's mean.

RILEY
It's adjacent. You asked about the tower. I'm answering the
adjacent question.

RUBELLA
What's the adjacent question.

RILEY
Whether you came up here to see metal or to see if the day would
change if you walked long enough.

RUBELLA
(long beat)
Both.

RILEY
Good. Then we're walking correctly.
```

## RR-D — The Padlock Is a Mood

**Placement:** Gate, Beat C. Three-handed Stoppard at the ordinance sign.  
**Pitch:** The cut lock as existential performance.

```
LILLITH
(reading)
"Violators subject to citation."

RUBELLA
Subject to. Not "will receive." Language hedges.

RILEY
Paren b. Enforcement discretion. There's always enforcement
discretion. It's how a cut lock gets re-hung so a moving vehicle
feels legal.

LILLITH
You said citation. Not felony.

RILEY
Correct. Felony is what people say when they want the lock to mean
more than a lock means.

LILLITH
Does the lock know it was cut.

RILEY
The lock is brass. It knows tension and release. It doesn't know
March. I know March. Somebody cut it in March and hung it back
because guilt is easier to carry if the gate looks official.

RUBELLA
Who.

RILEY
Somebody who wanted to drive up without waiting for November.
Somebody who understood paren b better than paren a. I didn't
ask their name. I wrote down the hardware.

LILLITH
And we're going through.

RUBELLA
(already opening the gate)
We were always going through. The sign is a mood.

RILEY
The sign is ordinance twelve dash one fourteen. The mood is yours.

LILLITH
If we don't close it behind us, did we ever open it.

RILEY
The gate doesn't care. Close it if you want. Nobody in this episode
will. That's not a rule I was told. That's a pattern I've noticed.

RUBELLA
(getting in)
Patterns are the only religion I trust.

RILEY
Patterns are how you predict Darren's truck. Get in before Lillith
starts charging for implications.
```

## RR-E — Head Gasket vs Timing Chain

**Placement:** Hood up, Beat E. Under the hood metaphysics.  
**Pitch:** Drama vs accuracy as moral philosophy.

```
LILLITH
Is it the head gasket.

RILEY
No.

LILLITH
It sounds like the head gasket. In movies, the head gasket is when
the marriage ends.

RILEY
It's a lower radiator hose. Split at the clamp. The engine is
fine. The marriage is fine. The hose is not fine. Hoses are where
this story lives now.

LILLITH
Why does everyone want it to be the head gasket.

RILEY
Because a head gasket is a narrative. Timing chain is maintenance.
People prefer narratives. They can cry at narratives. You cry at
a clamp and the clamp doesn't notice.

LILLITH
Will it get us up.

RILEY
No. It will get us down. Down is also a direction. Down is how
the sedan retires with dignity.

LILLITH
So we walk.

RILEY
So you walk. I already planned on walking. The pack was always the
plan. The sedan was a variable.

LILLITH
Variables make me nervous.

RILEY
Variables are how you know you're not in a script. In a script the
hose holds until the third act.

RUBELLA
(holding one boot)
Is this the third act.

RILEY
It's five-twenty. Acts are a fiction. Grades are real. Put the boot
on. Leave the heel. The image will last longer than the act break.
```

## RR-F — The Silence Wager

**Placement:** No Other Human Sounds — Beat B/C, the twenty-second walk and after.  
**Pitch:** If a tree falls in a north-face wood and nobody performs dialogue, is it still the wordy episode?

```
RUBELLA
(whispering)
Are we allowed to talk.

RILEY
(normal voice)
You're talking.

RUBELLA
I mean — is this the part where we're supposed to be a silent show.
I felt the channel change. I felt it go procedural and then I felt
it remember.

RILEY
The woods don't have a channel. The woods have absorption. Closed
canopy. No wind. Sound hits the needles and dies. That's physics.
Not programming.

RUBELLA
So we can talk.

RILEY
We can. It costs more here. Every word uses up air the trees
aren't giving back. That's why the rally starts short. Not because
someone wrote a rule. Because the room charges rent.

RUBELLA
What's a rally.

RILEY
You say something. I say something. We don't overlap. We don't
confess. We pass the ball because passing is cheaper than holding.

RUBELLA
Who serves.

RILEY
You asked about the tower. You served. I'll get a turn later. I
always get one turn. That's not arrogance. That's structure.

RUBELLA
What if I don't want structure. What if I want —

RILEY
Then you're in the wrong mile. The graded fork is west. Lillith took
the sedan. Structure took the sedan. You took the trees.

RUBELLA
(long beat)
Your turn.

RILEY
Why did you get out of the car.

RUBELLA
It had expressed itself.

RILEY
That's a mechanical opinion, not a reason.

RUBELLA
I don't like being the one who waits.

RILEY
You waited in lace on a grade. Waiting is a skill. I don't have
it. I walk. Different filing systems.

RUBELLA
If we stop talking right now — completely — for eight seconds,
will the episode punish us.

RILEY
I don't know what an episode is. I know if we stop for eight
seconds, you'll hear your own blood. The woods will not help.
That's not punishment. That's a room with the door shut.

(They walk. Eight seconds. No score.)

RUBELLA
(quiet)
It heard us.

RILEY
No. You heard you. That's the wager. You lost. Keep walking.
```

## RR-G — One Boot, One Stiletto, One Will

**Placement:** Turnout, boot exchange. Three voices, footwear as free will.  
**Pitch:** Did you choose the boot, or did the grade choose for you?

```
RUBELLA
(holding a trail boot like it's evidence)
Explain this.

RILEY
It's a size seven. Salomon. Resoled once. I keep one spare pair
because lace is not a tread pattern.

LILLITH
Why one boot each.

RILEY
Because you each brought one failure of footwear and I brought the
correction. Division of labor. You didn't pack for eleven percent.
That's not my ordinance.

RUBELLA
I had a choice.

RILEY
You had a stiletto. Choice came later, when the heel met gravel and
gravel won.

LILLITH
I choose the left boot.

RUBELLA
I choose the right.

RILEY
You don't choose. You put on what fits the foot you have. Free will
is overstated on fire roads. Gravity is not.

LILLITH
(two experimental steps uphill)
This is humiliating.

RILEY
This is traction. Humiliation is when you slide backward in front
of a repeater you can't reach.

RUBELLA
(one boot, one heel, walking)
We look absurd.

RILEY
You look like a woman who refused to let a mountain win a footwear
argument. That's not absurd. That's committed. Absurd is driving
up in lace without a backup plan.

LILLITH
We had a plan.

RILEY
You had a sedan. Plans have hose clamps.

RUBELLA
If we make it to the top like this — one boot one heel — does it
mean something.

RILEY
It means you walked. Meaning is what people add when the grade
was enough by itself.
```

## RR-H — Whether Smith Is Adequate

**Placement:** No Other Human Sounds — Beat C/D, last name once.  
**Pitch:** A name as coin flip — adequate, adequate, adequate.

```
RUBELLA
I don't know your name.

RILEY
You didn't ask.

RUBELLA
I'm asking.

RILEY
Riley.

RUBELLA
That's a first name. I have a first name. Everyone has a first
name. It's not a gift. It's a label on a mug.

RILEY
Elaine if you're filling out a form. I don't use Elaine. Forms are
not this conversation.

RUBELLA
Last name.

RILEY
Smith.

RUBELLA
Smith.

RILEY
Yes.

RUBELLA
That's —

RILEY
Adequate. I know. It's exactly as adequate as it sounds. My mother
kept hers. My father kept his. Darren kept his. June will keep
hers. It's a family tradition of adequate.

RUBELLA
You say it like you lost a coin flip.

RILEY
I didn't flip. I was born into a county full of Smiths and one
wind chime missing a note since 2007. Adequate is fine. Adequate
gets the mail. Adequate is on the site list I'm not supposed to
have.

RUBELLA
Would you have preferred something that meant something.

RILEY
Names don't mean. They point. Smith points at me the way 4N22 points
at a road that stopped being graded in 2011. Still permitted. Still
there.

RUBELLA
Riley Smith.

RILEY
Once per episode. You spent it. Keep walking.
```

## RR-I — What the Tower Is Waiting For

**Placement:** Pulpit Overlook or pre-switchback — tower offscreen, discussed only.  
**Pitch:** The antenna as absent Hamlet.

```
RUBELLA
Is it watching us.

RILEY
It's not watching. It doesn't have eyes. It has a red lamp that
may or may not pulse. That's not watching. That's aviation law.

LILLITH
Could it be transmitting.

RILEY
Could is a big word for a decommissioned weather repeater on a ducting
weekend. Could is how people turn metal into plot.

RUBELLA
What if it's the EyeWash Station.

RILEY
Then you're in a different show. I'm logging a tower. You're
welcome to log a feeling.

LILLITH
Don't be smug.

RILEY
I'm not smug. I'm accurate. Smug is when you're right and you
linger. I don't linger. I write it down and move.

RUBELLA
What's the finding.

RILEY
Still there. Same finding as 2019. Same finding as ninety-eight.
The finding is always still there until someone pays to take it
down, and nobody pays.

LILLITH
So we're walking toward a thing that will not change.

RILEY
You're walking toward a thing that will not change on your schedule.
The sun will change. The ducting will change. The fire will change,
if you stay long enough and you're careless with a stove. The tower
will creak. That's the whole repertoire.

RUBELLA
Fire.

RILEY
You brought a lace dress to a ridge. Fire is always on the table.
I'm not threatening you. I'm describing August.

LILLITH
(long beat)
Let's walk.
```

## RR-J — The Fire We Have Not Lit

**Placement:** Saddle / blue hour — before the song. Meta-waiting.  
**Pitch:** Characters know something is supposed to happen and refuse to hurry it.

```
RUBELLA
Shouldn't something happen now.

RILEY
We're standing on a saddle. That's something. The grade ended. Your
calves know. That's data.

LILLITH
She means narratively.

RILEY
I don't know what that means. The sun is leaving. The tower is
black against the sky. You wanted the top. You're at the top. If
you need a bell to ring, I don't have one.

RUBELLA
In other episodes something explodes.

RILEY
This episode talked. Talking episodes get silence at the end. That's
the trade. You spent the budget.

LILLITH
How long do we wait.

RILEY
Fourteen seconds is traditional. Not my tradition. Yours. I only
learned it by riding in your car.

(They stand. Wind. Cicadas stop.)

RUBELLA
(whisper)
Is this the silence.

RILEY
Yes.

LILLITH
It's working.

RILEY
It's not working. It's happening. Don't congratulate the weather.

(Fourteen seconds. Nobody speaks.)

RUBELLA
Now what.

RILEY
Now you listen. Or you sing. Or you light a fire you'll regret.
Those are the options. They're not in order. They're in August.

LILLITH
If we light it.

RILEY
Then the tower gets a neighbor for the night. And a red lamp might
pulse. And nobody will look up. That's the episode. You were in it.
```

## RR-K — Who Decelerated First (fragment)

**Placement:** Teaser alt — pure tennis.

```
LILLITH
Who moved first.

RILEY
The road moved. The road is eleven miles of moving. I was already
moving. You were moving faster until you weren't. Deceleration is
a confession.

LILLITH
Confession of what.

RILEY
Interest. You slowed because I looked like a question you hadn't
finished asking.

LILLITH
And if we hadn't slowed.

RILEY
Then I'd be at mile marker four by now and you'd be a sedan in a
story I wasn't in. Deceleration is how you entered the story. Don't
litigate entry. You're already in.
```

## RR-L — Ordinance as Scripture (fragment)

**Placement:** Gate — paren theology.

```
RUBELLA
Read paren c.

RILEY
Enforcement discretion.

RUBELLA
Read it like you believe it.

RILEY
(reading flat)
"Enforcement discretion may be exercised by authorized personnel
in cases of hazard, emergency, or—"

RUBELLA
Go on.

RILEY
There's no go on. It's discretion. The point is they can choose.
Choice is why the lock is lying. The lock is a performance of law.
The cut behind it is the truth. Both can exist. That's paren b's
gift.

LILLITH
You're preaching.

RILEY
I'm citing. Preaching is when you don't have the number.
```
