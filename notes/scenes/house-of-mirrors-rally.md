# We Play to Win (House of Mirrors Rally)

*Long-form dialog scene — The Two. A full emotional rally: swap, score, boredom as love language.*

**Slugline:** INT. ABANDONED BALLROOM — NIGHT
**Runtime target:** 4–5 min (≈5 script pages)
**Music cue:** Pendulum rhythm — low piano, synth arpeggio on a slow swing, no drums. The ping-pong ball IS the metronome; let the score follow the rally, not the other way around. Build under Liliane's monologue, drop out entirely for the mascara beat, return under the button. Palette refs: *Twilight Time* pad, *The Long Goodbye* piano.
**Status:** draft
**Dialog versions (alts):** [`versions/house-of-mirrors-rally-versions.md`](versions/house-of-mirrors-rally-versions.md)


**Cast:** VIVIENNE VALE · LILIANE VALE · PIZZA GUY (final beat, one line)

**Spine:** A rally. Not a conversation — a rally. Vivienne proposes the swap; Liliane accepts on terms Vivienne didn't offer; both keep score; neither wins, which is the point.

**Emotional notes:** Never flustered. Chaos happens *to* others; they observe it. Catastrophe is a scheduling inconvenience. Everything here is stylized, loaded, or performative — they have not had a "normal" conversation since the gala. Their stillness is the direction; keep parentheticals minimal. The only true thing said in this scene is said by accident, twice, and neither of them acknowledges it.

**Sentence-finishing rule:** Liliane finishes Vivienne's sentences **incorrectly, on purpose.** In this scene it escalates from habit → sport → weapon → (once) mercy. Track it: Beat B (habit), Beat C (sport), Beat E (weapon), Beat F (mercy). That progression is the actual plot.

### A — Serve

```
INT. ABANDONED BALLROOM — NIGHT

A luxury ruin. Parquet buckled into slow waves. Water stain across
the ceiling fresco in the shape of a continent nobody would want.

A chandelier hangs low — too low, three feet lower than a chandelier
should hang, its chain having made a decision sometime last decade.
It is lit. Nobody has asked how.

Under it: a ping-pong table. Regulation. Immaculate. Wrong.

On a side table: two glasses of red, a bowl of berries, a paddle nobody
is using, and a small leather-bound notebook, closed.

VIVIENNE serves. LILIANE returns. The rally starts and does not stop
for the length of this scene. Every line in Beat A lands on a beat of
the ball.
```

```
VIVIENNE
Tonight we swap lives.

LILIANE
We did that.

VIVIENNE
We did *April.*

LILIANE
April counted.

VIVIENNE
April was a weekend.

LILIANE
April was eleven days and a funeral.

VIVIENNE
(pleased)
Whose?

LILIANE
Yours. You were very moved.

VIVIENNE
I remember. I wore the grey.

LILIANE
You wore my grey.

VIVIENNE
I wore the grey *available to me at the time*, which was in your
closet, which was, that week, my closet.

LILIANE
(returning)
And the eulogy?

VIVIENNE
Extemporaneous.

LILIANE
It was four minutes long and it rhymed.

VIVIENNE
Grief takes people differently.
```

```
The ball goes long. Vivienne does not chase it. It rolls into the
dark at the edge of the room and keeps going for an unlikely length
of time.

Liliane produces another from somewhere in the lace.
```

### B — Terms (Habit)

```
INT. BALLROOM — CONTINUOUS

The rally resumes. Liliane's returns are all slightly harder than
they need to be. Neither acknowledges this.
```

```
VIVIENNE
New terms.

LILIANE
There are always new terms.

VIVIENNE
Full swap. Names, keys, correspondence, the standing Thursday.

LILIANE
The Thursday.

VIVIENNE
You've wanted the Thursday for two years.

LILIANE
I've wanted you to *offer* the Thursday for two years. Those
are different appetites.

VIVIENNE
Then take it.

LILIANE
Not yet. What's it cost.

VIVIENNE
Nothing. It's a gift.

LILIANE
(returning)
It's never—

VIVIENNE
—a gift, I know. And also the debts.

LILIANE
There it is.

VIVIENNE
There are four. Two are decorative.

LILIANE
And the other—

VIVIENNE
—two are load-bearing, yes.
(beat)
You were going to say "the other two are yours."

LILIANE
I was going to say "and the other woman."

VIVIENNE
(a very small pause — she loses half a step and covers it)
That's a third category.

LILIANE
That's the whole ledger, darling. You just call it filing.
```

### C — Score (Sport)

```
INT. BALLROOM — CONTINUOUS

Liliane sets down her paddle mid-rally. The ball hits the table, hits
the floor, and nobody minds. She picks up her wine instead.

She does not open the notebook. She never opens the notebook.
```

```
VIVIENNE
Point.

LILIANE
Nine.

VIVIENNE
Nine what.

LILIANE
Nine to six. Since Tuesday.

VIVIENNE
We aren't keeping score.

LILIANE
*You* aren't keeping score. That's not the same as we.

VIVIENNE
(taking her own wine)
Go on then. Itemize me.

LILIANE
You interrupted a man's proposal to ask where he got the
lighting. Two points. You told the ambassador's wife her
house was "brave." One. You did not laugh when I fell down
the east stairs, which — 

VIVIENNE
Which was restraint.

LILIANE
Which was *timing*, and worth two, and I gave them to you.
You have been given points, Vivienne. That should frighten
you more than it does.

VIVIENNE
And your six?

LILIANE
Four for the gala. One for the thing with the dog.

VIVIENNE
And the sixth.

LILIANE
(drinking)
The sixth is pending.
```

```
Somewhere above them, a beam gives — a long, ligneous complaint that
resolves into a crack. Plaster dust sifts down through the chandelier
light like weather in a snow globe.

Neither looks up. Vivienne moves her wine three inches to the left.
The plaster lands where the wine was.
```

```
VIVIENNE
Finally. Natural ventilation.

LILIANE
It's been asking for months.
```

### D — Vivienne's Monologue

```
INT. BALLROOM — CONTINUOUS

Vivienne walks the long edge of the table, trailing one finger on it,
the way you'd walk the rail of a ship you intended to leave.
```

```
VIVIENNE
I'll tell you why I want the swap, and then you'll say
something cruel, and then we'll go on.
(beat)
I woke up Tuesday and I knew what was in the post before I
opened it. All of it. Three invitations, a summons, and a
thank-you card from a woman who has never thanked anyone.
I knew the wording of the thank-you card. Not the sentiment —
the *wording.* I said it out loud into the room before I
opened the envelope and I was correct, and there was nobody
there to be impressed.

(she stops at the net)

That's the thing nobody warns you about. Not the fires. Not
the debts. Not the men in the driveway with their little
folders. It's that you get good enough at a life that the
life stops being a life and becomes a *forecast.*

I don't want your money, darling. I have money; it's the least
interesting thing either of us has. I don't want your house —
your house has a smell. I want to open a letter I can't
predict. I want to walk into a room and not know who's already
decided to hate me.

(beat)

I want a Tuesday that isn't a rerun.

So: full swap. Starting now. You be the one who knows what's
in the post. I'll be the one who finds out.
```

```
Silence. Liliane lets it go three beats too long — a punishment she
administers with perfect neutrality.
```

```
LILIANE
Your house has a smell too.

VIVIENNE
(satisfied; this is exactly what she ordered)
I know.
```

### E — Liliane's Monologue (Weapon)

```
INT. BALLROOM — CONTINUOUS

Liliane crosses to Vivienne's side of the table. This is a violation
of every rule they have ever had.
```

```
LILIANE
No.

VIVIENNE
No?

LILIANE
Not "no, never." "No, and here's the invoice."
(beat)
You've told me that speech before. Not the words — you're too
vain to repeat words. The *shape.* You get bored, you propose
a swap, you describe the boredom beautifully so that the swap
sounds like philosophy, and I take your life for a season and
find out that what you actually wanted was for somebody else
to answer the phone.

I have been you four times. Do you know what's in your post,
Vivienne? I do. There's a letter from your mother that you
have not opened since March, and there's a second one under
it, and there's a third. That's what's in your post. That's
the Tuesday you can't predict, and it's been sitting on your
hall table predicting itself at you for five months, and you'd
rather burn a town down than slide a knife under the flap.

(beat)

You want to be the one who finds out. Fine. Be that. But
you've never once been the one who finds out. You're the one
who *leaves the room* so someone else finds out and brings it
to you already survived.

(beat)

I'll take the swap. Names, keys, the Thursday, the four debts,
the third category. All of it. On one condition.

VIVIENNE
Say it.

LILIANE
You open the letters first. All three. Tonight. Before you
hand me the keys.
(beat)
I'm not asking you to read them. I'm asking you to open them,
so that when I'm you, I'm not the one holding a life with a
locked room in it.
```

```
The chandelier swings, very slightly, in a room with no wind.
```

### F — Don't Smudge the Mascara (Mercy)

```
INT. BALLROOM — CONTINUOUS

Vivienne does not answer for a long moment.

Something in her face moves. Not much — the equivalent, on her, of a
building coming down. Her eyes are bright in a way that is not about
the chandelier.

Liliane sees it. Liliane has been waiting years to see it, and now
that it's here, she does not want it.

She reaches up and, with one knuckle, turns Vivienne's chin a quarter
inch toward the light. It is the tenderest gesture either of them has
made on camera.
```

```
LILIANE
(quietly)
Don't smudge the mascara.

VIVIENNE
(not moving)
That's what you have to say.

LILIANE
That's what I'm *able* to say.
(beat)
It's the same sentence, darling. It's just the version that
fits through the door.

VIVIENNE
(a breath; the building stops coming down)
...I'll open them.

LILIANE
Tonight.

VIVIENNE
Tonight.

LILIANE
And I'll — 

VIVIENNE
(finishing it, and finishing it *correctly*, for the
first time all night)
—be there when I do.

LILIANE
(a beat; this costs her)
I was going to say "and I'll pour."

VIVIENNE
(taking her wine, and Liliane's)
You were not.
```

```
Above them, the beam finishes what it started in Beat C. A section of
ceiling — six feet across, plaster and lath and one gilt cherub —
lets go and lands on the far end of the ping-pong table.

The table splits. The chandelier keeps burning. Neither woman spills.
```

```
LILIANE
(regarding the wreckage, mildly)
Point.

VIVIENNE
Nine to seven.

LILIANE
Nine to seven.
```

### G — Button: Order For

```
INT. BALLROOM — DOORWAY — CONTINUOUS

Three knocks on a door that has no business still being on its hinges.

THE PIZZA GUY. Red uniform. Perfect posture. One box. Behind him, a
corridor of mirrors receding into the dark, each reflection of him
very slightly more composed than the last.

He does not react to the collapsed ceiling. He does not react to the
chandelier. He steps over a cherub.
```

```
PIZZA GUY
Order for... Viviane?

(Neither woman corrects him.)

LILIANE
(taking the box)
She'll have the arugula.

VIVIENNE
She'll have the regret.

PIZZA GUY
Extra napkins are in the bag.
```

```
He goes. Down the mirror corridor. The reflections take slightly
longer to leave than he does.

Liliane sets the box on the unbroken half of the table. Vivienne
picks up a berry. Neither of them opens the box, tonight or ever.

Wide. Two women in black lace, a split ping-pong table, a hole in the
ceiling with stars in it, a chandelier that has outlived the building
it hangs in.

Hold.
```

```
VIVIENNE
(without looking over)
Nine to seven.

LILIANE
For now.
```
