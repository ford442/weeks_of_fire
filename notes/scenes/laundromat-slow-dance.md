# 2AM Laundromat Slow Dance

*Long-form dialog scene — musical cutaway. The song does the confession the characters won't.*

**Slugline:** INT. 24-HOUR LAUNDROMAT — 2:00 AM
**Runtime target:** 3–4 min (≈3½ script pages)
**Music cue:** **"The Laundromat Saints"** — original Minimax track, diegetic from the corner TV, then blooming past diegetic. Structure tags below in Beat D. Sibling energy: *Empty Parking Lot Waltz*. Slow, warm, slightly wrong; brushed kit, wet upright piano, one dryer's off-balance thump usable as the actual percussion bed.
**Status:** draft

**Cast:** HER and HIM — the LAUNDROMAT SAINTS (never named, on screen or in the credits) · NIGHT ATTENDANT · CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O., corner TV, optional)

**Spine:** Two strangers almost tell the truth. The attendant mops around them. The song says the thing, and then the dryer buzzes, and they let it go.

**Emotional notes:** The silences are the script. They are written, timed, and non-negotiable — do not let a performer fill one. Nobody in this scene is lonely in a way they'd admit to; they are both *available*, which is worse. Warmth, not melancholy. Nothing is consummated and nothing is wasted.

**Casting note:** Credit them as SAINT (1) and SAINT (2) or as nothing at all. **They do not exchange names, on screen or off.** If a draft gives either of them a name, the draft is wrong.

### A — Two People and a Machine That Isn't Level

```
INT. 24-HOUR LAUNDROMAT — NIGHT

Fluorescent. All of it. The kind of light that makes everyone look
like they're being processed.

Twelve washers, eight dryers. Seven of the dryers are asleep. One is
not: it turns with a THUMP on every rotation, off-balance, patient,
like a heart with an opinion.

Rain on the front glass. Beyond it, an empty street that occasionally
admits a pair of headlights and then reconsiders.

A TV mounted in the corner, bracket-hung, angled at nobody. It is
showing EyeWash Station. Nobody has ever changed this channel.

HER: mid-30s, a wool coat over something that was formal eight hours
ago. Half a Halloween's worth of glitter still on one cheekbone, the
way glitter refuses.

HIM: 40s, wrinkled work uniform, name patch turned inward or torn off
— we never read it.

They are four machines apart. Both watching the off-balance dryer,
because it is the only thing in the room doing anything.

THE ATTENDANT (any age; fluorescent vest; headphones with one ear off)
is mopping a floor that is clean.

Nobody speaks for eleven seconds. Time it. Eleven.
```

```
HIM
That one's not level.

HER
No.

(five seconds)

HIM
They put a shim under it in June.

HER
Did it work?

HIM
(watching it thump)
It worked in June.
```

```
(Eight seconds. She almost says something. She revises it in her
face. The revision is the performance.)
```

```
HER
It's kind of nice.

HIM
The thump?

HER
(nodding)
Something in here's keeping time.
```

### B — The Overlap (Almost-Dialog)

```
INT. LAUNDROMAT — CONTINUOUS

They have drifted two machines closer. Neither decided to. Both would
say the other one did.

What follows is a full page of two people failing to say a true thing.
Play it OVERLAPPING — starts stepped on, apologies stepped on,
restarts stepped on. Nobody finishes. Nobody minds. The rhythm is a
rally with no ball.
```

```
HER
Can I ask you some—

HIM
You're not from—

(both stop)

HER
Go ahead.

HIM
No, you.

HER
I forgot it.

HIM
(a beat, believing her, which is generous)
You're not from the Tuesday crowd.

HER
There's a Tuesday crowd?

HIM
There's a Tuesday crowd. Nurses, mostly. And a guy who reads.

HER
What does he read?

HIM
I've never asked. That's the arrangement.
(beat)
Sorry — the arrange—

HER
No, I get it. I get the arrange—

(both stop)

(Six seconds. The dryer thumps four times.)

HER (CONT'D)
I came here because I didn't want to go home yet, and I ran
out of places that would let me stand still.

HIM
Yeah.

HER
That's — 
(she hears how much she just said)
That's a lot of laundry, is what I meant.

HIM
(letting her have it)
It's a lot of laundry.

(four seconds)

HIM (CONT'D)
I do mine on Thursdays. Usually.

HER
And tonight?

HIM
(long enough that the answer is the pause)
Tonight I did mine on Thursday and came back anyway.

HER
Oh.

HIM
Yeah.

HER
(a small laugh, not at him)
So you're — your clothes are clean.

HIM
My clothes are immaculate.
```

```
(Nine seconds. She reaches into her basket and starts folding a
towel. Then another one. He watches her fold two towels, and then,
because there is nothing else to do with his hands, he picks up the
end of the sheet she can't manage alone, and they fold it together
without discussing it: corner to corner, walk in, hand off, step
back, walk in.

They fold three things. It is the most intimate thing either of them
has done in a year and they get away with it because it's laundry.)
```

### C — The Attendant

```
INT. LAUNDROMAT — CONTINUOUS

The ATTENDANT arrives at their feet with the mop and does not ask
them to move. Mops around them. Around the basket. Around the sheet.
The mop goes between them and they lift their feet without breaking
the fold.

The Attendant speaks. This is the only thing the Attendant says in
the entire scene, and it is delivered to the floor, at the volume of
somebody who has never once needed to be listened to.
```

```
ATTENDANT
Machine four takes the dollar coin, machine six takes the card
reader if you hold it there, machine nine don't. Bleach is
behind the counter but I'm not getting it after one.
(mopping)
People come in around now. Two, two-thirty. Not for the
machines — the machines are open at noon and nobody's here at
noon. They come in now because it's the only room in town
that's lit up and don't want anything.
(mopping)
You don't have to buy anything. You don't have to be anybody.
You put your stuff in the drum and you sit there for an hour
and the hour goes by whether or not you figure it out.
(beat; still to the floor)
That's the whole thing. The hour goes by anyway. Might as
well be warm.

(He mops on. Past them. Gone.)

(Seven seconds.)

HER
(quietly)
Did he just—

HIM
Don't.

HER
Okay.

HIM
He'll do it again next week. He doesn't know he's doing it.

HER
(watching him go)
That's the only way anybody ever does it.
```

### D — The Song (Slow Dance)

```
INT. LAUNDROMAT — CONTINUOUS

The corner TV is airing a music slot. EyeWash Station's 2 AM block.
The card behind the song reads RINSE CYCLE.

The song is small and tinny for eight bars. Then the mix quietly
stops pretending it comes from the TV, and the room has a score.

Neither of them moves for a long moment.

Then she puts down the towel.
```

```
HER
You don't have to.

HIM
I know.
(stepping in)
That's why I'm doing it.
```

```
They dance. Badly at first — genuinely badly, two people negotiating
whose hand goes where in a room with no music etiquette. Then it
settles, the way it does, into something plain and serious.

They dance between the rows. Steam from an open dryer moves through
the fluorescents like weather that got indoors.

The off-balance dryer keeps time. The song lets it.

No dialogue for the length of a verse and a chorus. Not one word.
Their faces do the work. Shoot it in two shots and stay wide once.
```

### E — The Buzzer

```
INT. LAUNDROMAT — CONTINUOUS

The song is in its last four bars.

The off-balance dryer BUZZES. Loud, flat, industrial, entirely without
tact.

The song does not finish. It gets cut off mid-phrase by the buzzer,
which is correct.

They stop. They do not step back immediately, which is the whole
performance.
```

```
HIM
That's me.

HER
Okay.

(four seconds)

HIM
I'm here most—

HER
Don't.

HIM
(nodding; he agrees, and he's relieved)
Yeah.

(three seconds)

HER
Thursdays.

HIM
Usually.

HER
(picking up her basket)
Usually.
```

```
He opens the dryer. Warm air rolls out into the fluorescents. He
begins to fold, and it is the first badly folded thing in this
laundromat all night.

She goes to the door. Stops. Doesn't turn around.

Beat.

She goes.

The bell over the door. The rain. The street.

He folds. The ATTENDANT mops past behind him and does not look up.

On the corner TV: a card. WE NOW CONCLUDE OUR BROADCAST DAY.
```

```
CONTINUITY VOICE (V.O.)
(small, from a five-inch speaker)
We'll return to your program.
(beat)
We always do.
```

```
Hold on the room. Seven dryers asleep. One warm. Rain.

Hold three seconds longer than is comfortable. Then out.
```
