## Series sonic bible

Every Minimax track in *Weeks on Fire* is secretly airing on EyeWash Station,
and it should sound like it: warm, slightly worn, and mixed like a transmission
rather than a record. Three rules hold across the catalog.

1. **Nobody in these songs is acting.** The Two are bored, the Pizza Guy is on
   schedule, the Continuity Voice is sincere, the driver is half asleep. No
   belting, no big pop vibrato, no winking. Emotion arrives through
   arrangement — an instrument entering, a room changing — never through a
   performance getting louder.
2. **Something is always structurally wrong, and it is never corrected.** A bar
   too long, a chord that lands late, an arpeggio that fails, a phrase that
   answers a beat behind itself. One deliberate flaw per track, held for the
   whole runtime.
3. **Keep the top end dark and the space real.** Tape hiss, mains hum, room
   reverb, and a field recording under the bed (rain, wipers, fluorescent
   buzz, distant traffic). Clean digital brightness is reserved for the
   celestial tracks (*Strange Light*, *Porch Light Meridian*) — that contrast
   is the point.

Voices: **Rubella** for glamour and drift, **Lillith** for the cabaret duets
and whispered leads, a deadpan **baritone** for the Pizza Guy, and a warm
institutional announcer for anything the station says about itself.

## Song Idea: "Empty Parking Lot Waltz"

**Working Title:** Empty Parking Lot Waltz (or "Last Cars Out")

**Genre / Vibe / Style References:** Slow cinematic waltz / dark cabaret, 3/4 time, minor key with major lifts in the chorus. Think early Tom Waits meets a haunted music box. Very sparse arrangement — upright piano, brushed drums or soft hand percussion, distant upright bass, one haunting female or androgynous vocal with lots of space and reverb. Almost no high frequencies until a single swelling string or glass harmonica moment near the end.

**Key Instruments & Production Notes:** 
- Solo piano as the main voice (slightly detuned for melancholy)
- Very light brushed snare or finger-on-cymbal texture
- Distant traffic or parking lot wind noise as "field recording" bed
- Vocal recorded with a single close mic, almost whispered in places
- One big dynamic swell around 2:15 where the "orchestra" (or a single violin) enters for 8 bars then vanishes

**Lyrical Themes / Hook / Chorus Ideas:** 
The last two people in a massive empty parking lot after some kind of event (concert? disaster? both?). They're not quite ready to leave. The waltz is about circling each other, car keys in hand, deciding whether to say the real thing or just drive away.

Hook idea:
"We could go / or we could stay / and let the streetlights watch us pretend / there's still somewhere to be"

**Emotional Arc or Story Fit:** Starts intimate and hesitant, builds to a quiet revelation, ends on an unresolved "maybe next time" that feels both hopeful and devastating. Perfect for a post-chaos / post-party cutaway (pairs with the Velvet Rope or Morning After scenes).

**Potential Episode / Scene Placement:** Musical cutaway button for Episode 3 or 4, or standalone short film. Could score the final pull-back shot in a "last cars leaving the neighborhood" visual.

**Grok Imagine Visual Companion Concepts:** 
- Two cars alone in a vast wet parking lot at 1am, their headlights the only lights
- A woman in a rumpled dress leaning against her car door, singing to herself
- Reflections of streetlights in puddles forming a grid of stars
- Slow orbiting camera move around the two figures as they almost dance without touching

**Reference Tracks / Artists:** 
- "Waltz (Better Than Fine)" — Fiona Apple (the intimate late-night feel)
- "The Last Waltz" — Engelbert Humperdinck (but much sadder)
- Any Daniel Lanois pedal steel ballad slowed down 20%

**Status:** (Concept → Lyrics draft → Ready for Minimax generation → Integrated) Strong concept. Ready for a full lyric draft + Minimax prompt. Would work beautifully with Rubella-style vocal.

## Song Idea: "Recycling Bin Rebellion"

**Working Title:** Recycling Bin Rebellion (or "Wrong Shade of Blue")

**Genre / Vibe / Style References:** Absurd punk-ska / suburban protest anthem. Fast, bright, slightly ramshackle. Think early Rancid meets They Might Be Giants meets a marching band that got lost in a cul-de-sac. Horns (trumpet + trombone), snappy snare, distorted electric guitar, group "hey!" gang vocals on the chorus. The energy of people who are extremely mad about something objectively silly.

**Key Instruments & Production Notes:** 
- Bright, slightly cheap-sounding horns (the "HOA meeting room" sound)
- Snare rimshots like someone tapping a gavel
- Gang vocals that sound like 6 tired neighbors who have been drinking
- One "breakdown" where everything drops to just a single off-key trumpet playing the "take me home" melody from the HOA Muzak

**Lyrical Themes / Hook / Chorus Ideas:** 
A song about the small rules that become existential battles when the world is ending. The chorus is an absurdly sincere chant about recycling bin color violations while purple rifts open in the sky.

Hook:
"It's the wrong shade of democratic blue! / It's anarchy blue, Karen, and I won't have it in my cul-de-sac!"

**Emotional Arc or Story Fit:** Starts as petty bickering, escalates into full glorious rebellion, then ends with everyone calmly going back inside when the pizza guy shows up again. The absurdity is the point. Comedic relief that still has a little melancholy underneath (these people are trying so hard to keep the world normal).

**Potential Episode / Scene Placement:** Perfect musical cutaway or end-credits song for the HOA episode (Episode 4 concept). Could also underscore the "Morning After the Portal" scene.

**Grok Imagine Visual Companion Concepts:** 
- Wide shot of seven adults in folding chairs screaming the chorus while a glowing portal spins behind the whiteboard
- A recycling bin being carried like a battle standard
- The HOA president crowd-surfing (impossible in the small room, yet happening)
- Final shot: everyone sitting back down, adjusting their name tags, as if nothing happened

**Reference Tracks / Artists:** 
- "Birdhouse in Your Soul" — They Might Be Giants (the cheerful absurdity)
- "Linoleum" — NOFX (the suburban punk energy)
- Any Spinal Tap song (for the over-the-top commitment to the bit)

**Status:** (Concept → Lyrics draft → Ready for Minimax generation → Integrated) Very fun. Would be a great contrast to the more melancholic or atmospheric tracks in the series. High comedic value for live review screenings.

## Song Idea: "Strange Light" — Minimax Music 3.0 attempts

**Working Title:** Strange Light

**Why this song fits the show:** Sun and moon sharing one sky maps cleanly onto Episode 3’s final crane — suburban chaos below, something stranger above the moonlit sky. It also echoes Episode 2’s half-conscious wonder (dual glow, held breath, nobody else noticing) without needing Halloween lasers. Quiet cosmic lawbreaking; one witness; a color you carry home.

**Noah Cohn comparison (https://open.spotify.com/artist/3qflv1JHTbpGMbmjZO1wWo):** Your catalog is progressive / downtempo / ambient electronica with celestial titles (*Stella Porta*, *Meridian*, *Wisdom of the Sky*, *Silence En Orbite*, *Stellar Day*) and a long-form, patient build language (SH-101 / MS2000 / acid-adjacent synth heritage, chillout and progressive house lean). Best overlap = keep the pure sine bass as “Meridian pulse,” add slower pad beds and gradual filter evolution à la *Levity*-era downtempo, and let vocals float rather than front like pop radio.

### Strange Light — generation tips for Music 3.0
