**Marble Tick Tack — magnetics pass (cylinders, jumps, mutual orbit, more dancers)**

Second punch-in pass on the same 2:03, same three rooms, same JSON id. The orbit pass got magnets
moving *around* things; this is what the takes still do not have: magnets moving **around each
other**, magnets **crossing empty space to hit each other**, and much more of the lot **vibrating
on the grid** while they do it.

**Cylinders join the body list.** Discs stand on their flat ends and spin like tops at 136 RPM
with the axis wobbling slowly against the tick; rods roll on their sides and pair end-to-end into
dumbbells that tumble end-over-end *while travelling*; disc stacks counter-rotate, alternating
discs turning opposite ways at once. Spheres cannot do any of that.

**The jump gap** — the one the pass is really for. A magnet sits dead still across a visible gap,
then crosses it in a single frame and slams. Rules, because a model will happily turn this into a
roll or a cut:

- show the gap first, three to six body-widths, held a full beat
- both bodies dead still before it — stillness is the wind-up
- cross on one frame, a streak, nothing in between
- land the impact on the **68 BPM kick**, never an eighth
- pay it: grit puff, one skid, then a **rebound ring** over about four eighths
- never cut inside the jump, one per clip

Homes: **B** (doorway, the small first one), **D** (alley, the big one, straight into the
non-contact gears), **L** (cabinet, the jump that starts the Gauss chain).

**Mutual orbit** at **K** — two *equal* bodies rotating around the empty point between them, with
nothing in the middle, while the whole rotating pair travels home up the rut. Neither one is the
center. If Imagine puts a big sphere in the middle and gives it a moon, kill the take.

**Dancer bank** — far more objects on the eighths so the rooms move, not just the props. Cabinet:
marbles in the grooves, wine-glass rims ringing without moving, a pull-chain ticking, washers on
a nail, ball bearings in a tin lid, a saucer of water with crossing rings, shelf dust in the lamp
beam. Alley: fence slats, frost on wire, grit hopping in the ruts, chain-link buzzing, a
corrugated sheet oil-canning on the kick, a hubcap ringing, a bare bulb swinging. Lawn: dew beads
jumping off the blades, flower heads nodding on the kick, grass tips shivering, coil housings
humming, standing water in a footprint. Three or four per clip, never all — everything shaking
reads as an earthquake, not a beat.

Indoors the dancers are glass and metal, in the alley dry and gritty, on the lawn wet. That is how
the rooms stay apart when the magnets are doing the same job in all three.

**The rule for the whole pass: dancers hop, magnets orbit.** Furniture never travels. If a fence
slat starts orbiting something, kill the take.

Generate order: **D jump gap first** (highest risk in the piece), then **K mutual orbit**, then
**A disc stack**, then **G dumbbell tumble**, dancer layers last.
